## 1. AQI Above 300: What It Means

The Air Quality Index (AQI) is a standardized tool measuring air pollution levels and their impact on public health. An AQI reading above 300 signifies **hazardous air quality**. At such levels, the air is severely polluted and the risk of adverse health effects increases significantly for everyone, including healthy individuals.

### AQI Severity Categories (India CPCB Scale)

| AQI Range | Category | Health Impact | What to Do |
|-----------|----------|---------------|------------|
| 0–50 | Good | Safe for everyone | Normal outdoor activity |
| 51–100 | Moderate | Minor risk for very sensitive individuals | Sensitive groups limit prolonged outdoor activity |
| 101–150 | Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups | Mild to moderate symptoms for sensitive groups | Sensitive groups limit outdoor exposure; avoid strenuous exercise |
| 151–200 | Unhealthy | Breathing problems likely for those with respiratory/cardiovascular conditions; healthy individuals may notice discomfort | Limit outdoor activities; wear N95 if outdoors unavoidable |
| 201–300 | Very Unhealthy | Serious risk for everyone; severe difficulties for chronic patients | Stay indoors; use air purifiers; seek medical attention if symptoms persist |
| Above 300 | Hazardous | Immediate and severe health effects for entire population; risk of chronic respiratory disease, heart attacks, strokes, and premature death | Stay strictly indoors; seal windows; run air purifiers continuously; emergency care for severe symptoms |

## 2. Why AQI Surges Above 300 in Urban Areas

Factors causing AQI to spike above 300 in metro regions:

- **Industrial emissions** from factories and power plants
- **Vehicular exhaust** from millions of vehicles on congested roads
- **Construction dust** from ongoing infrastructure development
- **Crop burning** in neighboring agricultural states (especially Punjab and Haryana)
- **Weather conditions**: Temperature inversions trap pollutants near ground level; winter fog reduces wind dispersal
- **PM₂.₅ concentration**: Fine particles (< 2.5 microns) penetrate deep into lungs and enter the bloodstream — a major driver of hazardous AQI in cities

**Important note:** Individual health impact can vary greatly based on:
- Pre-existing health conditions
- Age (children and elderly most vulnerable)
- Proximity to pollution sources
- Duration of exposure

## 3. Who Is Most at Risk When AQI Crosses 300

### Children and Teenagers
- Respiratory systems still developing — more vulnerable to irritants
- Exposure to high AQI leads to long-term respiratory issues, allergies, and developmental delays in lung function
- Teenagers who are active outdoors face higher risk through increased breathing rate during exercise

### Elderly Adults
- Weakened immune systems and pre-existing conditions (asthma, COPD, heart disease) amplify vulnerability
- Risk of **heart attacks, strokes, and respiratory failure** increases significantly with hazardous air
- Must stay strictly indoors during AQI > 300 episodes

### Heart and Lung Patients
- PM₂.₅ irritates lung tissue and worsens asthma
- Increased likelihood of cardiac events for those with COPD, emphysema, or ischemic heart disease
- Must carry rescue medication (inhalers, cardiac medications) at all times during high AQI periods

### Pregnant Women
- Poor air quality can affect oxygen levels reaching the fetus
- Associated with **premature births, low birth weight, and developmental problems**
- Increased oxidative stress may lead to pregnancy complications

## 4. Common Health Symptoms Seen in Hazardous Air Quality (AQI > 300)

### Respiratory Symptoms
- **Breathing difficulty and wheezing**: Particulate matter causes airway constriction, leading to shortness of breath and wheezing — especially in those with asthma
- **Coughing and chest tightness**: Body's attempt to expel harmful particles; persistent coughing and chest discomfort are warning signs

### ENT Symptoms
- **Irritated eyes, nose, and throat**: Redness, itching, dryness, and scratchy throat from PM exposure
- People with allergies experience significantly worsened symptoms

### Systemic Symptoms
- **Dizziness and fatigue**: Compromised oxygen supply from high pollutant concentrations causes lightheadedness, tiredness, and even fainting
- **Headaches** from poor oxygenation and toxic gas exposure

## 5. When to Seek Immediate Medical Attention

**Seek emergency care immediately if:**
- Severe shortness of breath that does not resolve when resting
- Persistent chest pain or pressure
- Dizziness, fainting, or loss of consciousness
- Rapid worsening of asthma or COPD symptoms despite using rescue inhaler
- Blue lips or fingertips (cyanosis — sign of oxygen deprivation)

Conditions like asthma attacks or heart problems can worsen **rapidly** in hazardous air quality. Do not delay medical consultation.

## 6. Immediate Steps to Reduce Outdoor Exposure

### Avoid Peak Traffic Hours
- Peak traffic pollution windows: **7:30–9:00 AM** and **5:00–7:30 PM**
- Avoid outdoor activities during these windows when AQI is above 300
- If travel is unavoidable, minimize time spent outside during peak hours

### Postpone Outdoor Workouts
- Do not run, cycle, or walk outdoors during hazardous AQI
- Exercise increases breathing rate, increasing pollutant inhalation significantly
- Respiratory issues from outdoor exercise in poor AQI can cause lasting damage

### Choose Indoor Physical Activity
- Yoga, stretching, light exercises
- Ensure indoor space is sealed from outdoor pollutants before exercising indoors
- Indoor exercise with open windows on a high-AQI day offers little benefit over outdoor exercise

### Choosing the Right Mask for AQI > 300
- **N95 respirator**: Filters 95% of airborne particles including PM₂.₅; recommended for AQI 200–300
- **N99 respirator**: Filters 99% of airborne particles; preferred for AQI above 300
- Mask must fit snugly covering both nose and mouth — no gaps allowed
- Both N95 and N99 protect against PM₂.₅ effectively; surgical and cloth masks do not

## 7. How to Improve Indoor Air Quality at Home

### Use Air Purifiers with HEPA Filters
- HEPA (High-Efficiency Particulate Air) filters remove PM₂.₅ and other fine particles
- Essential in cities with AQI above 300 during winter months
- Place purifiers in bedrooms and main living areas; run continuously during poor AQI periods
- Keep filters clean and replace as per manufacturer schedule

### Reduce Indoor Pollution Sources
- Do not burn candles, incense, or tobacco indoors during high AQI periods
- Avoid smoking indoors entirely
- Use exhaust fans while cooking to remove cooking fumes
- Regularly clean home surfaces using damp microfibre cloths to prevent particle re-suspension

### Control Humidity Levels
- Maintain humidity at **30–50%** to reduce mold, dust mites, and particle suspension
- Use a dehumidifier if indoor humidity exceeds 60%
- Do not allow indoor humidity to drop below 30% (encourages airborne particle movement)

### Proper Ventilation Timing
- Open windows only when outdoor AQI drops below 100 (typically midday in winter)
- Avoid opening windows in early morning and late evening during smog season
- When ventilating, allow fresh air from higher floors if possible (less road-level pollution)

### Air-Purifying Indoor Plants
- **Snake Plant (Sansevieria)**: Absorbs PM₂.₅, benzene, and formaldehyde; low maintenance
- **Peace Lily (Spathiphyllum)**: Improves air quality; removes VOCs
- Place plants in bedrooms and living spaces as supplementary (not replacement) air filtration

## 8. Precautions at Work and School During AQI > 300

### At the Workplace
1. **Limit outdoor activities**: Move outdoor meetings and breaks indoors; cancel outdoor site inspections where possible
2. **Improve indoor air quality**: Deploy HEPA air purifiers; keep windows sealed during poor AQI hours
3. **Protective gear**: Provide N95 masks to employees who must work outdoors
4. **Medical support readiness**: Ensure availability of first aid for respiratory distress
5. **Flexible work arrangements**: Offer remote work options or adjusted hours to reduce outdoor commute exposure

### At Schools
1. **Cancel outdoor sports and recess**: Move physical education classes indoors with lower-intensity activities
2. **Equip classrooms**: Install air purifiers or seal windows from polluted outdoor air
3. **Distribute protective masks**: Especially for students with respiratory conditions
4. **Teacher monitoring**: Train teachers to recognize signs of respiratory distress and provide quick access to medical care
5. **Virtual learning options**: Consider online classes on days when air quality is extremely poor (AQI > 400)

## 9. Protective Measures for Children and Senior Citizens

**For children:**
- Keep indoors as much as possible during AQI > 300
- Avoid all physical exertion outdoors
- Use air purifiers in bedrooms during sleep
- Monitor closely for coughing, wheezing, or shortness of breath
- Have asthma inhalers or bronchodilators readily accessible

**For senior citizens:**
- Stay indoors; limit even indoor movement if symptoms are present
- Keep prescribed cardiac and respiratory medications easily accessible
- Ensure adequate hydration
- Avoid exposure to cooking smoke, incense, or indoor irritants
- Consult doctor proactively at the start of high-pollution seasons

## 10. Diet and Hydration Tips During Hazardous AQI

**Antioxidant-rich foods** help combat oxidative stress caused by air pollution:
- **Berries** (blueberries, amla): high in antioxidants; neutralize free radicals
- **Green leafy vegetables** (spinach, kale): support detoxification
- **Nuts** (walnuts, almonds): Omega-3 fatty acids and Vitamin E reduce inflammation
- **Citrus fruits** (oranges, lemon, guava): Vitamin C boosts immune and respiratory defense

**Hydration:**
- Drink **plenty of water** to help flush pollutants and toxins from the body
- Herbal teas (ginger, tulsi) provide additional anti-inflammatory protection

**Foods to avoid during smog season:**
- Fried and oily foods (increase systemic inflammation)
- Cold drinks (may aggravate throat and airway irritation)

## 11. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can AQI above 300 trigger breathing problems in healthy adults?**  
Yes. AQI above 300 can cause breathing difficulty, coughing, and throat irritation even in healthy adults due to the high concentration of PM₂.₅ and other pollutants.

**Q: What masks are best when AQI crosses 300?**  
N95 or N99 respirators are best. They filter 95–99% of airborne particles including PM₂.₅. Surgical and cloth masks are not effective against fine particulate matter.

**Q: Do air purifiers really help during severe pollution?**  
Yes. Air purifiers with HEPA filters significantly improve indoor air quality by removing harmful particles like PM₂.₅, reducing health risks associated with poor outdoor air quality.

**Q: How to treat bad air quality symptoms at home?**  
Stay indoors, use a humidifier, drink plenty of fluids. For persistent symptoms: use a nebulizer (if prescribed), or consult a pulmonologist for appropriate medications like bronchodilators.

**Q: How to protect children from hazardous air pollution?**  
Keep children indoors on AQI > 300 days, limit all outdoor activities, use air purifiers in their rooms, ensure they wear N95 masks if they must go outside, and monitor for respiratory distress symptoms.
