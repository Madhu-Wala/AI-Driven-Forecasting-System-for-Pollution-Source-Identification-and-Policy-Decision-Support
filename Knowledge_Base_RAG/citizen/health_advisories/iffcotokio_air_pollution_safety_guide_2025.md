## 1. Health Hazards of Air Pollution

Prolonged exposure to polluted air causes both short-term discomfort and long-term health issues across multiple body systems:

### Respiratory Issues
- Air pollution irritates the airways, leading to coughing, wheezing, and breathlessness
- Worsens existing asthma and bronchitis symptoms significantly
- Even brief exposure during severe AQI events can cause measurable respiratory impairment

### Chronic Lung Diseases
- Continuous exposure to pollutants may lead to **COPD (Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease)**
- Long-term exposure to fine particulate matter and toxic gases is linked to **lung cancer**

### Cardiovascular Effects
- Pollutants (especially PM₂.₅) can enter the bloodstream, increasing risk of **heart attacks and strokes**
- Risk is heightened during smog season when PM₂.₅ and PM₁₀ levels peak
- Long-term exposure leads to sustained vascular inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, and plaque buildup in arteries

### Weakened Immunity
- Long-term exposure reduces the body's natural defense system
- Makes individuals more prone to infections — viral and bacterial
- Compounded effect during winter when respiratory infections are already more prevalent

### Allergies and Sinusitis
- Dust, pollen, and suspended particles may trigger allergic reactions and sinusitis
- Already sensitised individuals experience significantly worse symptoms during high AQI days

### Eye Irritation
- Itchy, burning, or red eyes are common during high pollution days
- In severe cases, prolonged exposure may lead to eye infections (conjunctivitis)

## 2. Outdoor Precautions

### Check Air Quality Regularly
- Use real-time AQI apps (AQI India, IQAir, CPCB Sameer) daily before going outdoors
- **Avoid stepping outdoors when AQI exceeds 300** (Severe category)
- Check AQI at your specific neighbourhood level — city-average AQI may not reflect your local conditions
- Set up app notifications to alert you when AQI crosses preset thresholds (e.g., 200)

### Wear an N95 or N99 Mask
- Choose certified masks that filter **PM₂.₅ and PM₁₀ particles**
- Avoid surgical or cloth masks — they are less effective against fine particulate matter
- Ensure the mask creates an airtight seal around the nose and mouth
- Dispose of single-use masks safely after use; replace reusable mask filters per instructions
- During very poor AQI (300+), prefer **N99 masks** for superior filtration (99% particle removal)

### Limit Outdoor Activities
- Avoid running, jogging, cycling, and other high-exertion outdoor activities when pollution is high
- High-intensity activity increases breathing rate, dramatically increasing pollutant inhalation
- Opt for **indoor workouts, yoga, or stretching** on poor AQI days
- If outdoor activity is necessary, choose the least polluted time of day (typically 11:00 AM – 3:00 PM in winter)
- Morning walks between 5:00–9:00 AM and evening walks between 6:00–10:00 PM should be avoided during smog episodes

### Cleaner Transportation Choices
- Use **public transport, carpooling, or electric vehicles** to reduce personal emission footprint
- Taking public transport also reduces time spent in high-traffic emission zones
- Regularly maintain your vehicle to reduce emissions (ensure timely service, clean air filters, PUC compliance)

### Avoid Burning Waste
- **Never burn dry leaves, plastics, or garbage** — they release highly toxic fumes and worsen local air quality
- Report illegal waste burning to BMC or local municipal authority
- Burning any material outdoors significantly contributes to neighbourhood-level PM₂.₅ spikes

## 3. Indoor Precautions

### Use Air Purifiers with HEPA Filters
- Install a purifier with a **HEPA filter** to remove fine particles (PM₂.₅ and PM₁₀)
- Run purifiers continuously during high AQI periods, especially during nighttime
- Keep filters clean and replace them as per manufacturer's instructions
- Ensure purifier capacity matches room size (check CADR rating)
- Priority rooms: bedroom, children's room, living room

### Add Air-Purifying Indoor Plants
Plants that help cleanse indoor air naturally:
- **Snake Plant (Sansevieria)**: Absorbs PM₂.₅, benzene, formaldehyde; low maintenance; can be kept in bedroom
- **Spider Plant**: Removes carbon monoxide and nitrogen dioxide effectively
- **Aloe Vera**: Absorbs benzene and formaldehyde; doubles as household medicinal plant
- **Peace Lily (Spathiphyllum)**: Removes VOCs; thrives in low light
- **Areca Palm**: Humidifies air naturally; filters multiple pollutants

Note: Plants are a **supplement to**, not a replacement for, air purifiers during severe AQI events.

### Ensure Proper Ventilation (Time It Right)
- Avoid opening windows early morning or late evening when smog settles close to ground level
- Best ventilation window: midday (11:00 AM – 2:00 PM) when AQI is typically at its lowest during winter
- Use exhaust fans in kitchen and bathroom to improve air circulation
- Install chimney filters in kitchen to prevent cooking fumes from adding to indoor PM levels

### Stay Hydrated and Eat Right
**Hydration:**
- Drink plenty of water and herbal teas to help flush toxins from the body
- Hot water with ginger and honey helps soothe irritated airways

**Protective foods:**
- **Vitamin C** (oranges, amla, guava, lemon): boosts respiratory immunity; helps neutralize free radicals from pollution
- **Omega-3 fatty acids** (flaxseed, walnuts, fish): reduce systemic inflammation triggered by PM₂.₅
- **Vitamin E** (almonds, sunflower seeds): protects cell membranes from oxidative damage

**Foods to avoid during smog season:**
- Fried and oily foods (increase inflammatory load)
- Cold beverages (may aggravate throat irritation)

### Keep Indoors Dust-Free
- Regularly clean floors, curtains, and furniture using **damp cloths** (dry dusting disperses particles into the air)
- Use vacuum cleaners with **HEPA filters** for more effective dust removal
- Change or wash bedding frequently during high AQI periods
- Remove shoes at the door to avoid tracking in particulate matter

## 4. Medical Precautions

### Consult a Doctor
- Seek medical advice for **continuous coughing, wheezing, or chest tightness** that persists beyond a day or two
- People with asthma or heart conditions should **keep rescue medications readily accessible** (inhalers, bronchodilators, nitroglycerin) at all times during high pollution periods
- Do not self-medicate with antibiotics for pollution-aggravated viral respiratory infections

### Steam Inhalation and Nasal Irrigation
- Regular **steam inhalation** (with eucalyptus or peppermint oil added to hot water) helps:
  - Clear congested airways
  - Soothe inflamed nasal passages
  - Remove particulate matter lodged in nasal passages
- **Saline nasal sprays or nasal irrigation (Neti pot)** help flush out pollutants trapped in nasal passages
- Perform 1–2 times daily during prolonged high-pollution periods

### Protect Your Eyes
- Wear **UV-protective glasses** when outdoors on polluted days — they reduce particulate matter exposure to eyes
- Use **lubricating eye drops** to prevent dryness and irritation from PM exposure
- Avoid rubbing eyes (spreading pollutants from hands to eyes)
- If eye irritation is severe or persistent, consult an ophthalmologist

### Get Vaccinated
- **Flu vaccine**: reduces risk of influenza during winter months when air pollution weakens respiratory defenses
- **Pneumonia vaccine**: recommended for elderly and immunocompromised individuals; offers protection against respiratory infections during pollution-heavy months
- Vaccines do not protect against pollution itself but reduce vulnerability to infections that are more severe when combined with poor air quality

## 5. Smart Habits for 2025

### Smart Air Quality Monitoring
- Install portable **smart air quality monitors** at home to track:
  - Real-time indoor PM₂.₅ levels
  - VOC concentrations
  - Humidity and temperature (affect particle behaviour)
- Set automatic alerts when indoor AQI exceeds a safe threshold
- Recommended monitoring locations: bedroom and main living area

### Smart Home Integration
- Connect your air purifier to **smart home systems (Alexa, Google Home)** for:
  - Automatic activation when indoor AQI exceeds a preset threshold
  - Remote monitoring and control
  - Scheduled operation during predicted high-pollution hours

### Mindful Breathing and Yoga
- Practice **Pranayama** (yogic breathing exercises) to strengthen lung capacity:
  - **Anulom-Vilom** (alternate nostril breathing): balances breathing and strengthens lung function
  - **Bhramari** (humming bee breath): reduces stress and supports respiratory health
  - **Kapalbhati** (skull-shining breath): improves lung elasticity (consult doctor if you have respiratory conditions)
- Deep breathing exercises (done indoors with purified air) help maintain lung health during sustained pollution episodes

## 6. Quick Reference: Air Pollution Safety Checklist

| Situation | Recommended Action |
|-----------|-------------------|
| AQI 0–100 | Normal activities; light precautions for sensitive groups |
| AQI 100–200 | Limit prolonged outdoor activity for sensitive groups; monitor regularly |
| AQI 200–300 | Avoid strenuous outdoor exercise; wear N95 if going out; close windows |
| AQI 300+ | Stay indoors; run air purifiers; seal windows; N99 mask if outdoors unavoidable |
| Respiratory symptoms develop | Consult doctor; do not self-medicate with antibiotics |
| Children/elderly in household | Higher level of precaution at all AQI levels above 100 |
| Winter smog season | Daily AQI monitoring; preemptive indoor air preparation |
