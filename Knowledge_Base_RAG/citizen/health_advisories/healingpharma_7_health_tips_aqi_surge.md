## 1. Context: Why AQI Surges Happen

Every year after the festival season (Diwali and post-harvest period), AQI surge levels in India frequently cross the "Severe" category, making air unsafe for all age groups.

**Compounding factors during AQI surges:**
- Firecracker emissions from festive celebrations
- Stubble/crop residue burning in northern agricultural states
- Vehicle pollution in urban areas
- Cooler weather conditions that trap harmful particles near ground level (low atmospheric mixing)
- Winter temperature inversions that prevent upward dispersion of pollutants

**Resulting health complaints even in healthy individuals:**
- Respiratory discomfort
- Eye irritation
- Cough
- Skin itching
- Fatigue

### AQI Scale Reference
| AQI Level | Category | Action Required |
|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| 0–100 | Good to Satisfactory | Normal activity |
| 101–200 | Moderate | Caution for sensitive groups |
| 201–300 | Poor/Unhealthy | Limit outdoor exposure |
| 300+ | Very Poor/Hazardous | Stay indoors; wear N95/N99 |

## 2. Seven Expert-Recommended Health Protection Tips

### Tip 1: Monitor AQI Before Stepping Outside

**Knowledge is the first line of defense.** Make it a daily habit to check AQI levels before going outdoors.

**How to monitor:**
- Use smartphone apps: AQI India, CPCB SAMEER app, IQAir
- Check government portals: safar.iitm.ac.in, app.cpcbccr.com
- Set up app alerts for when AQI in your area crosses a specific threshold (e.g., 200)

**Actions based on AQI reading:**
- AQI in "Very Poor" or "Severe" range: **Limit outdoor exposure entirely**
- Avoid early morning walks or late evening jogs — these are peak pollution times
- Postpone outdoor workouts or switch to indoor exercises instead
- If you have identified local patterns (e.g., Tuesday mornings are worse due to heavy truck traffic), use that knowledge to plan

---

### Tip 2: Create a Safe Indoor Environment

When the air outside is toxic, your home should be your haven. Indoor air can also become polluted if ventilation is poorly managed.

**Steps to maintain healthy indoor air:**

**Close and seal:**
- Close windows and doors during high-smog hours (typically early morning and late evening)
- Use draft stoppers under doors to prevent outdoor air infiltration

**Purify indoor air naturally:**
- Add indoor plants: **Snake Plant, Peace Lily, and Areca Palm** are known to absorb pollutants and increase oxygen levels
- Clean surfaces and floors regularly to prevent dust accumulation
- Use a microfibre cloth for dusting (not dry sweeping, which disperses particles)

**Maintain indoor humidity:**
- Keep a small bowl of water near air vents or use a humidifier
- Humidity helps trap dust particles and prevents them from becoming airborne
- Optimal humidity: 30–50%

**Ventilation strategy:**
- Ventilate rooms only when outdoor AQI improves (typically midday in winter)
- Avoid opening windows in early morning or during evening rush hours

### Tip 3: Choose the Right Mask

When stepping outside is unavoidable, protect your lungs with a high-quality mask.

**Why cloth and surgical masks are insufficient:**
- Cloth and surgical masks are not designed to block fine particulate matter like PM₂.₅
- They do not create an airtight seal around the face
- Fine particles can pass through the gaps

**Recommended mask types:**
- **N95 masks**: Filter up to **95% of airborne pollutants** including PM₂.₅
- **N99 masks**: Filter up to **99% of airborne pollutants** — preferred for AQI above 300
- **Reusable masks**: Choose eco-friendly options with replaceable HEPA-grade filters

**Proper mask usage:**
- Ensure the mask seals tightly around the nose and mouth without gaps
- Perform a seal check before going outside
- Replace disposable masks regularly; replace filters in reusable masks per manufacturer guidelines
- Avoid touching the mask surface while wearing

### Tip 4: Eat Right and Stay Hydrated to Fight Pollution Internally

Polluted air increases oxidative damage in the body. Antioxidant-rich foods help neutralize the effects of pollution-induced oxidative stress.

**Anti-pollution diet recommendations:**

**Vitamin C-rich foods (boost respiratory immunity):**
- Amla (Indian gooseberry) — highest natural source of Vitamin C
- Oranges, guava, kiwi, lemon
- Consumption supports immune function and combats inflammation

**Anti-inflammatory foods:**
- **Turmeric** (curcumin): natural anti-inflammatory; add to warm milk or food
- **Ginger**: reduces airway inflammation; beneficial as tea
- **Omega-3 fatty acids**: walnuts, flaxseeds, fish — reduce systemic inflammation

**Leafy greens for detoxification:**
- Spinach, kale, coriander — support liver detoxification from pollutant exposure

**Hydration:**
- Drink at least **8–10 glasses of water daily** during high pollution periods
- Water and fluids help flush toxins from the system
- Herbal teas (ginger, tulsi, peppermint) offer additional anti-inflammatory benefits

### Tip 5: Protect High-Risk Individuals Proactively

Certain individuals are more vulnerable to AQI surges and require heightened precautions:

**Children (highest risk):**
- Children breathe faster than adults and take in proportionally more air relative to body weight
- This significantly increases their dose of air pollutants
- Keep children strictly indoors on days with AQI above 200
- Cancel outdoor school activities, sports, and events on high-AQI days
- Use air purifiers in children's bedrooms, running during sleep hours

**Elderly individuals:**
- Pre-existing conditions (heart disease, COPD, diabetes) amplify health risks
- Avoid all outdoor activity during AQI surges
- Ensure regular medication adherence, especially for respiratory and cardiac conditions
- Monitor for early warning signs: shortness of breath, chest tightness, increased cough

**People with asthma/COPD:**
- Keep prescribed inhalers and bronchodilators readily accessible
- Follow doctor-prescribed action plans if symptoms worsen
- Consider prophylactic nebulization during multi-day AQI surge events (consult doctor)

**Pregnant women:**
- Minimize all outdoor exposure
- Ensure indoor air quality is optimal with air purifiers
- Consult doctor if experiencing respiratory symptoms

### Tip 6: Maintain Physical Activity Safely

Exercise is essential for health, but outdoor workouts during AQI surges can cause more harm than good.

**Exercise guidelines during poor AQI:**
- **AQI < 100**: Outdoor exercise safe for most people
- **AQI 100–200**: Sensitive groups should exercise indoors
- **AQI 200–300**: All outdoor strenuous exercise should be avoided
- **AQI > 300**: All outdoor activity should be minimized; indoors only

**Safe indoor exercise options:**
- Yoga (especially pranayama breathing exercises to strengthen lungs)
- Anulom-Vilom (alternate nostril breathing)
- Indoor cycling or treadmill (doors and windows sealed)
- Stretching and resistance training
- Online workout videos, dance fitness

**Avoid:**
- Running or jogging outdoors during poor AQI
- Cycling near traffic during high pollution hours
- Morning walks between 5–9 AM (peak pollution window in winter)

---

### Tip 7: Build Community Awareness and Take Collective Action

Individual actions are essential but insufficient. Community-level actions amplify pollution reduction.

**Individual contributions to reducing pollution:**
- Avoid burning dry leaves, plastic, and garbage — these release highly toxic fumes locally
- Avoid burning firecrackers or support campaigns encouraging responsible use
- Choose public transport, carpooling, or cycling where possible
- Use electric vehicles; support EV transition in your community

**Advocate for cleaner air:**
- Report garbage burning or construction dust violations to local BMC ward offices
- Participate in community clean-air drives
- Push local leaders for more green spaces and tree plantation
- Encourage regular vehicle maintenance and PUC compliance in your neighbourhood

| Protection Layer | Action |

| Information | Check AQI daily before going outside |
| Physical barrier | Use N95/N99 mask when outdoors is unavoidable |
