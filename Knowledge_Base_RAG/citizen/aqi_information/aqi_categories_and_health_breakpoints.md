# AQI Categories and Health Breakpoints

**Source:** National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) Report, Section 4.3, Table 3, Page 12  
**Authority:** Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) / Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEF&CC)  
**AQI Launched:** April 2015 by the Prime Minister of India  
**Category:** Citizen — AQI Categories Reference  

---

## AQI System Overview

The National AQI is a tool for effective communication of air quality status to the public. It transforms complex air quality data of multiple pollutants into a **single index value** with a corresponding category name and colour code.

**AQI Sub-index Calculation:**  
- Sub-indices are calculated for each of the 8 pollutants as a **linear function of the measured ambient concentration**.
- Example: PM₂.₅ sub-index = 51 at 31 µg/m³; sub-index = 75 at 45 µg/m³; sub-index = 100 at 60 µg/m³.
- The **worst sub-index** (highest value) among all pollutants determines the **overall AQI**.

**Pollutants covered by AQI sub-index:**  
PM₁₀, PM₂.₅, NO₂, SO₂, CO, O₃, NH₃, Lead (Pb) — all 8 pollutants for which short-term (up to 24-hour) NAAQS are prescribed.

## TABLE 3: AQI Categories and Health Breakpoints

| AQI Range | Category | Colour | Associated Health Impacts |
|-----------|----------|--------|--------------------------|
| **0 – 50** | **Good** | Green | **Minimal Impact.** Air quality is satisfactory; air pollution poses little or no risk. |
| **51 – 100** | **Satisfactory** | Light Green / Teal | **May cause minor breathing discomfort to sensitive people.** Unusually sensitive individuals should consider reducing prolonged outdoor exertion. |
| **101 – 200** | **Moderate** | Yellow | **May cause breathing discomfort to people with lung disease such as asthma, and discomfort to people with heart disease, children, and older adults.** General public may experience discomfort with prolonged exposure. |
| **201 – 300** | **Poor** | Orange | **May cause breathing discomfort to people on prolonged exposure, and discomfort to people with heart disease with short exposure.** Sensitive groups should avoid outdoor activity; general public should reduce outdoor exertion. |
| **301 – 400** | **Very Poor** | Red | **May cause respiratory illness to people on prolonged exposure. Effect may be more pronounced in people with lung and heart diseases.** General public should avoid outdoor exertion; sensitive groups must remain indoors. |
| **401 – 500** | **Severe** | Dark Red / Maroon | **May cause respiratory effects even on healthy people, and serious health impacts on people with lung/heart diseases. Health impacts may be experienced even during light physical activity.** General public must stay indoors; emergency health advisory to be issued. |

## Health Impact by Sensitive Population Group

| AQI Category | General Population | Children | Elderly | Respiratory/Cardiac Patients |
|--------------|-------------------|----------|---------|------------------------------|
| Good (0–50) | No risk | No risk | No risk | No risk |
| Satisfactory (51–100) | No risk | Minor discomfort possible | Minor discomfort possible | Minor breathing discomfort possible |
| Moderate (101–200) | Possible discomfort with prolonged exposure | Breathing discomfort possible | Discomfort possible | Asthma and heart disease patients should limit outdoor time |
| Poor (201–300) | Breathing discomfort on prolonged exposure | Should limit outdoor activity | Should limit outdoor activity | Short exposure causes discomfort; must avoid outdoor exertion |
| Very Poor (301–400) | Respiratory illness risk on prolonged exposure | Must avoid outdoor activity | Must avoid outdoor activity | Serious risk; must stay indoors; seek medical advice if symptoms worsen |
| Severe (401–500) | Respiratory effects even on healthy people | Emergency restriction on outdoor activity | Emergency restriction on outdoor activity | Serious health emergency; immediate medical attention if affected |

## AQI and NAAQS Compliance Relationship

The AQI Good/Satisfactory range corresponds to pollutant concentrations **within or below NAAQS limits**:
- PM₂.₅ NAAQS 24h limit: **60 µg/m³** (corresponds to AQI = 100, upper boundary of "Satisfactory")
- PM₁₀ NAAQS 24h limit: **100 µg/m³** (corresponds to AQI = 100, upper boundary of "Satisfactory")

When AQI crosses 100 (enters "Moderate"), at least one pollutant has **exceeded its 24-hour NAAQS limit**.

## AQI Scale — Operational Notes for RAG System

- **AQI 280**: Category = Poor. PM₂.₅ approximately 105–115 µg/m³. Action: GRAP Category I measures active (Maharashtra framework).
- **AQI 350**: Category = Very Poor. PM₂.₅ approximately 160 µg/m³. Action: GRAP Category II measures active.
- **AQI 420**: Category = Severe. PM₂.₅ > 250 µg/m³. Action: GRAP Category III measures active — industrial closures required.
- **AQI > 450 persisting 48h+**: Category = Severe+/Emergency. PM₂.₅ ≥ 300 µg/m³. Action: GRAP Category IV — maximum restrictions.
