## 1. Understanding AQI and Its Health Significance

The Air Quality Index (AQI) is a critical indicator of air pollution levels that indicates how safe — or hazardous — the air is for daily activities. Poor AQI is more than just an environmental issue; it is a pressing public health concern.

### AQI measures the following pollutants:
- Particulate Matter (PM₂.₅ and PM₁₀)
- Ozone (O₃)
- Nitrogen Dioxide (NO₂)
- Sulfur Dioxide (SO₂)
- Carbon Monoxide (CO)

### India AQI Categories (CPCB/SAFAR Scale)

| AQI Range | Category | Health Implication |
|-----------|----------|--------------------|
| 0–49 | Good | Safe for everyone |
| 50–99 | Satisfactory | Minimal risk for sensitive groups |
| 100–199 | Moderate | Mild effects on sensitive individuals |
| 200–300 | Poor | Discomfort for all; serious for sensitive groups |
| 301–400 | Very Poor | Respiratory illness risk for general population |
| 401–500 | Severe | Health emergency; affects all age groups |
| 500+ | Severe+ | Extreme health emergency |

**Clinical context (Mumbai):** Mumbai has been seeing poor AQI levels especially in winter months. The northern regions of India (including Delhi) frequently experience "Very Unhealthy" or "Hazardous" AQI categories during winter, but Mumbai is increasingly affected as well.

## 2. Vulnerable Populations — Who Is Most at Risk?

Certain groups are more susceptible to health effects of poor AQI:

**Children and Adolescents:**
- Developing lungs make them vulnerable to long-term damage
- Children breathe faster than adults and take in more air relative to body weight
- Higher rates of childhood asthma, respiratory infections, and chronic bronchitis
- Neurological and developmental effects are possible with chronic exposure

**Elderly Individuals:**
- Existing conditions such as heart disease amplify risks
- Weakened immune systems increase susceptibility to infection
- Risk of heart attacks, strokes, and respiratory failure increases significantly

**Pregnant Women:**
- Poor air quality can impact both the mother and the unborn child
- Risk of premature births, low birth weight, and developmental problems in the fetus
- Increased oxidative stress may complicate pregnancy

**Outdoor Workers:**
- Prolonged daily exposure heightens risk of respiratory and cardiovascular conditions
- Limited ability to reduce exposure during working hours

**People with Pre-existing Conditions:**
- Asthma, COPD, and diabetes make individuals more susceptible to complications
- Even moderately high AQI levels can worsen symptoms, reduce lung function, and increase hospital visits

## 3. Short-Term Health Effects of Poor AQI

Exposure to poor air quality causes immediate and measurable health effects:

- **Chest pain and breathing difficulties**
- **Coughing** (persistent, dry cough from airway irritation)
- **Throat irritation** and dryness
- **Airway inflammation**
- **Wheezing** (especially in those with asthma or bronchitis)
- **Eye irritation** — redness, itching, burning sensation
- **Headaches** and dizziness
- **Fatigue** and general weakness

**At AQI 200–300:** Primarily impacts vulnerable groups (senior citizens, children, those with comorbidities)  
**At AQI 300–400:** Affects a wider population, leading to various respiratory infections  
**At PM₂.₅ > 120 µg/m³:** Considered dangerous; may cause severe health consequences including detrimental effects on respiratory and cardiovascular systems

---

## 4. Long-Term Health Effects

Chronic exposure to air pollution leads to serious, progressive health conditions:

- **Lung tissue destruction** and impaired lung function
- **Asthma development or worsening** — particulate matter can cause asthma even in previously healthy individuals
- **Chronic bronchitis** and COPD exacerbation
- **Increased risk of respiratory infections** (viral and bacterial)
- **Cardiovascular disease**: heart attacks, arrhythmia, and strokes
- **Hypertension** from sustained inflammation
- **Endothelial dysfunction** and plaque buildup in arteries
- **Lung cancer risk** (especially in non-smokers in urban areas)
- **Neurological effects**: children exposed to air pollution show higher rates of ADHD and behavioral disorders

### Expert Medical Observation (Mumbai Context)
Dr. Tanu Singhal, Consultant Paediatrician and Infectious Diseases Specialist, Kokilaben Dhirubhai Ambani Hospital, has observed increased viral infections among children and adults during Mumbai's poor air quality periods. Exacerbation of asthma and allergies is the most common condition seen. People with milder respiratory infections are at risk of developing severe forms due to poor air quality.

**Warning:** Self-medicating with antibiotics for viral infections aggravated by pollution does more harm than good. Antibiotics do not treat viral infections. Consult a pulmonologist for persistent symptoms.

## 5. Particulate Matter — Why PM₂.₅ Is Especially Dangerous

Particulate matter (PM) is among the most dangerous air pollutants. It consists of tiny particles that get trapped in the lungs:
- **PM₂.₅** (particles < 2.5 microns): small enough to enter the lungs AND the bloodstream
  - Causes respiratory and cardiovascular problems
  - Effects on children are especially alarming
  - PM₂.₅ > 120 µg/m³ is medically dangerous
- **PM₁₀** (particles < 10 microns): trapped in upper respiratory tract

## 6. Precautions for Citizens During Poor AQI

### Stay Informed
- Monitor local AQI conditions daily using apps or government websites (SAFAR, AQI India, CPCB Sameer App)
- Identify days and times to avoid outdoor activities in your specific neighbourhood
- Follow government advisories and adapt activities accordingly
- Keep track of AQI trends, not just single-point readings

### Reduce Outdoor Exposure
- Minimize outdoor activities, especially during early mornings and late evenings when pollution peaks
- Do not exercise outdoors on days with AQI above 200 — trade-off between exercise benefits and harm from polluted air leans toward harm in vulnerable individuals
- Prioritize indoor activities (yoga, stretching, home workouts) during high pollution days

### Mask Usage
- Wear masks during heavy pollution periods
- Regularly change masks during heavy pollution to prevent clogging
- **Consistent use of inhalers is crucial** for asthmatic individuals, particularly children, during outdoor activities
- Use N95 or N99 masks for effective PM₂.₅ filtration (cloth and surgical masks are insufficient)

### Respiratory Infections — Special Precaution
- **Refrain from exposure to poor air quality during active respiratory infections** — it may exacerbate symptoms significantly
- Seek medical help early; do not delay for persistent cough, difficulty breathing, chest pain, or dizziness

### Window and Ventilation Management
- Keep windows closed to reduce exposure to polluted air, especially during peak pollution hours
- Do not open windows in early morning or late evening during smog season

### Indoor Air Management
- Dust surfaces with a microfibre cloth (not dry dusting, which disperses particles)
- Use air purifiers with HEPA filters in bedrooms and living areas
- Regular health check-ups with a pulmonologist help manage and mitigate long-term health effects

---

## 7. When to Seek Immediate Medical Attention

Seek urgent medical care if you or a family member experience:
- Persistent cough lasting more than a few days
- **Breathing difficulty or shortness of breath**
- **Chest discomfort or chest pain**
- Wheezing
- Dizziness or fainting spells
- Significant worsening of existing asthma or COPD symptoms

If experiencing breathing difficulty, chest discomfort, or wheezing, consult the Department of Pulmonary Medicine for prompt diagnosis and treatment.
