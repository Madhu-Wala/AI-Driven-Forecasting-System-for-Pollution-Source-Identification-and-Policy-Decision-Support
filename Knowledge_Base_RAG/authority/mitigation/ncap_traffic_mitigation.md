# NCAP Mitigation: Transport Sector Emissions

**Source:** National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) Report, Section 7.5.4, Pages 37–40  
**Authority:** Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH), MoEF&CC, CPCB  
**Category:** Authority — Traffic and Transport Mitigation  

---

## 1. Source Description and Pollution Drivers

Vehicles are identified as a **major source of urban air pollution**, particularly for PM₂.₅, NOₓ, CO, and VOCs. Vehicular contribution is concentrated in urban centres where ground-level exposure is highest.

### Source Contribution Data (Delhi, IIT Kanpur Study 2015):

| Pollutant | Season | Vehicle Contribution |
|-----------|--------|---------------------|
| PM₁₀ | Winter | 19.7% |
| PM₁₀ | Summer | 6.4% |
| PM₂.₅ | Winter | 25.1% |
| PM₂.₅ | Summer | 8.5% |

**National Scale (TERI Inventory):**
- Transport: **4% of national PM₂.₅ emissions** — but concentrated in urban centers, making ground-level contribution significantly higher.
- Transport: **35% of national NOₓ emissions** — highest of any sector.

**Mumbai-specific:** Vehicular pollution accounted for **30.5% of emissions** in Mumbai (2019–20, SAFAR), doubled from the 2016–17 level.

### Key Emission Drivers:
- **MHCVs (Medium & Heavy Commercial Vehicles)**: constitute just 2.5% of total vehicle fleet but contribute **60% of vehicular pollution**.
- **Old vehicles (>10 years, pre-BS I)**: constitute 15% of fleet but **pollute 10–12 times more** than a new vehicle.
- Idling vehicles at congested junctions produce disproportionately high localized PM₂.₅ and NOₓ.
- DG sets contribute **7–18% of ambient air pollution** in non-attainment cities (see Industrial section for DG set measures).

## 2. Regulatory Framework

| Regulation | Key Requirement |
|------------|----------------|
| Bharat Stage (BS) IV norms | Mandatory nationwide from April 1, 2017 |
| Bharat Stage (BS) VI norms | Mandatory nationwide from April 1, 2020 (India leapfrogged BS-V) |
| BS-VI fuel sulphur content | Reduced from 50 ppm (BS-IV) to **10 ppm** (BS-VI) |
| PUC Certificate (Central Motor Vehicle Rules, 1989) | Every motor vehicle must carry valid PUC certificate after 1 year from first registration |
| PUC validity — 4-wheeled BS-IV+ vehicles | **1 year** |
| PUC validity — all other vehicles | **3 months** |
| FAME India (NEMMP 2020) | National scheme for adoption of hybrid and electric vehicles |

## 3. Mitigation Measures and Action Points

### 3.1 Emission Standards

**Action 1: Stringent implementation of BS-VI norms across all of India.**
- All new vehicles manufactured from April 2020 must comply with BS-VI standards.
- BS-VI significantly reduces PM₂.₅ and NOₓ from vehicle exhausts.
- Fuel quality must be maintained: sulphur content ≤ 10 ppm in petrol and diesel.

### 3.2 Green Mobility

**Action 2: Stringent implementation of National Biofuel Policy — ethanol blending of 20% in petrol and 5% biodiesel in diesel by 2030.**
- Current baseline (at NCAP formulation): ~2% ethanol in petrol, <0.5% biodiesel in diesel.
- Target: 20% ethanol blending in petrol, 5% biodiesel in diesel by 2030 (National Biofuel Policy, 2018).

**Action 3: Review and extension of Mass Rapid Transit (MRT) in cities/towns.**
- City action plans to assess MRT expansion feasibility in each non-attainment city.
- Modal shift to MRT reduces vehicular emissions proportionally.

**Action 4: Improvement and strengthening of inspection and certification (I&C) system for vehicles through extension of I&C centres.**
- MoRTH to develop model I&C centres in all states.
- Centres verify vehicle fitness, emission compliance, and PUC status.

**Action 5: Stringent implementation of PUC certificate through regular inspection and monitoring.**
- Every vehicle to carry valid PUC certificate; 4-wheeled BS-IV+ vehicles require annual renewal; others require 3-monthly renewal.
- Regular checking drives to be conducted by RTO + Traffic Police jointly.
- Vehicles without valid PUC to be challaned; commercial vehicles identified for fitness retesting.

**Action 6: Fleet modernization and retrofitment programmes with emission control devices.**
- Voluntary Vehicle Fleet Modernization Programme (V-VMP): offers tax benefits and discounts to junk old vehicles and replace with new ones.
- Priority target: MHCVs (which are 2.5% of fleet but generate 60% of vehicular pollution).
- Scheme estimated potential: **25%–30% reduction in vehicular emissions**.
- 20 vehicle scrapping centres to be set up nationwide (Ministry of Steel).

**Action 7: Reducing real-world emissions through congestion management.**
- Traffic police deployment at identified high-congestion intersections.
- Smooth traffic flow reduces vehicle idling, which is a disproportionate PM₂.₅ source at junctions.
- Operationalization of expressways and highways to divert non-destined through-traffic.

**Action 8: Review the Green Corridor Project (GAIL) for CNG availability beyond city limits.**
- Extend CNG availability to enable use of clean fuel for long-distance journeys.
- Assess feasibility of extension with reference to 102 non-attainment cities.

**Action 9: Review scaling up of MoPNG pilot project for CNG in 2-wheelers.**
- CNG-retrofitted two-wheelers being piloted — scale-up if successful.

**Action 10: Scaling up of R&D on hydrogen as transport fuel.**
- Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas: Hydrogen Corpus Fund of ₹100 crores established with five major oil companies + OIDB.

### 3.3 E-Mobility

**EV Emission Reduction Potential (studies cited in NCAP):**
- GHGs reduced by approximately **82%** over EV lifetime vs. gasoline vehicles.
- Local pollutants reduced: **36% for PM; 45% for SOₓ; 96% for VOCs; 81% for NOₓ; 99% for CO**.

**Action 11: Formulate national-, state-, and city-specific action plan for e-mobility.**

**Action 12: Rapid augmentation of EV charging infrastructure, focusing on 102 non-attainment cities.**
- Both standard AC charging and rapid DC charging infrastructure required.

**Action 13: Central government vehicle fleets older than 15 years to be shifted to electric vehicles.**

**Action 14: Government-run buses for public transport, private buses, and 3-wheelers to be converted to EVs.**

**Action 15: Gradual transition to e-mobility in the 2-wheeler sector.**

**Action 16: Investment in R&D — battery manufacturing indigenization, alternate battery chemistry (reducing lithium/cobalt dependency), circular economy for battery reuse and recycling.**
