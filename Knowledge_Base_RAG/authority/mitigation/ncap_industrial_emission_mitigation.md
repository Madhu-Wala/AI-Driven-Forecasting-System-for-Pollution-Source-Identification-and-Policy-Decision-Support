# NCAP Mitigation: Industrial Emissions

**Source:** National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) Report, Section 7.5.3 (Industrial Emission) and Section 7.5.2 (Power Sector), Pages 34–37  
**Authority:** MoEF&CC, CPCB, State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs)  
**Category:** Authority — Industrial Emission Mitigation  

---

## 1. Source Description and Pollution Drivers

Industrial emissions — including from factories, power plants, brick kilns, stone crushers, and DG sets — are a **major contributor to ambient PM₂.₅, SO₂, and NOₓ** in non-attainment cities.

### Source Contribution Data:

**National scale (TERI Inventory):**
- Industrial sector: **36% of national PM₂.₅ emissions**
- Residential combustion: **39% of national PM₂.₅ emissions**
- Power plants: **4% of national PM₂.₅ emissions** (but very high localized impact in zones of influence)
- SO₂ emissions: Industry **49%**, Power sector **43%**

**Mumbai specific:**
- Industrial activity: **18% of Mumbai's overall air pollution** (MPCB)
- Industrial contribution to Mumbai PM₂.₅: **26%** (Times of India / MPCB)

**Urban scale (IIT Kanpur Delhi study, 2015):**
- Industrial contribution to PM₁₀: Winter 0.65%, Summer 1.05%
- Industrial contribution to PM₂.₅: Winter 0.8%, Summer 1.2% (direct; secondary from industrial SO₂ and NOₓ is additional)

### Key Emission Drivers:
- **Industrial clusters** (SME zones) are major pollution hubs due to weak enforcement and awareness.
- **88 prominent industrial clusters** identified for Comprehensive Environmental Pollution Index (CEPI) analysis (CPCB, 2009):
  - **43 clusters** with CEPI score ≥ 70 designated as **Critically Polluted Areas (CPAs)**
  - **32 clusters** with CEPI score 60–70 designated as **Severely Polluted Areas (SPAs)**
- **DG sets**: 91% have no in-use regulations beyond point of manufacture; aging DG sets may emit **11 times** the manufacturer standards; contribute **7–18%** of ambient air pollution in non-attainment cities.
- Brick kilns and stone crushers: major local PM sources in and around cities; operational without meeting prescribed norms in many cases.

## 2. Regulatory Framework

| Regulation | Scope | Key Requirement |
|------------|-------|----------------|
| CPCB OCEMS Directive | 17 categories of highly polluting industries | Mandatory installation of Online Continuous Emission/Effluent Monitoring System (OCEMS) with connectivity to CPCB server |
| Industry-specific emission standards | 63 industrial sectors | MoEF&CC has notified standards for 63 sectors |
| Red/Orange/Green/White industry classification | All industries (SPCBs/PCCs) | Classification for enforcement prioritization; revised criteria adopted |
| CEPI framework (CPCB) | 88 industrial clusters | Regular CEPI scoring; CPAs (score ≥ 70) must have CAAQMS and third-party environmental monitoring |
| Air Act, 1981 — Section 18(1)(b) | SPCBs/PCCs in NCR + all non-attainment cities | OCEMS mandatory for red-category air-polluting industries in NCR (23 districts + Delhi) from December 2017 |
| CPCB Direction (November 2016) | Brick kilns | All brick kilns not meeting norms, not converted to zig-zag technology, or lacking valid consent closed until March 31, 2017 |
| Pet coke and furnace oil ban | NCR States | Ban on use of pet coke and furnace oil as fuel from October 24, 2017 |
| Imported pet coke ban | Country-wide | Ban from July 26, 2018 (exceptions for permitted processes only) |

**OCEMS Compliance Status (at NCAP publication):**
- 17 categories → 3,531 industry units total
- 2,743 units have installed OCEMS (compliance achieved)
- **740 non-complying units** have closure directions in force

## 3. Mitigation Measures and Action Points

### 3.1 Emission Standards and Fuel Switching

**Action 1: Introduction of gaseous fuels and enforcement of new and stringent SO₂/NOₓ/PM₂.₅ standards for industries using solid fuels.**
- All industries currently using coal or solid fuels must either switch to natural gas/clean fuels or comply with revised PM₂.₅, SO₂, NOₓ emission standards.
- CGD (City Gas Distribution) network expansion to be prioritized in 102 non-attainment cities to facilitate natural gas availability.

**Action 2: Stricter enforcement of standards in large industries through continuous monitoring.**
- OCEMS mandatory for 17 categories of highly polluting industries.
- OCEMS data to feed into CPCB central server for real-time monitoring.
- Third-party environmental monitoring mandatory in CPAs under revised CEPI framework.
- Environmental quality data including CEPI score must be placed in public domain by concerned SPCBs/PCCs.

### 3.2 Specific Industry Controls

**Action 3: Full enforcement of zig-zag brick technology in brick kilns.**
- All brick kilns must convert from natural draft to induced draft (rectangular kiln shape, zig-zag brick setting).
- Non-compliant brick kilns operating without valid SPCB consent to be closed.
- Zig-zag technology significantly reduces PM emissions from brick kilns compared to traditional clamps.

**Action 4: Control by innovative end-of-pipe control technologies.**
- Encourage adoption of bag filters, ESP (electrostatic precipitators), wet scrubbers, and cyclone separators as applicable to industry type.
- End-of-pipe control devices to meet minimum PM capturing efficiencies per CPCB notified standards.

### 3.3 DG Set Controls

**Action 5: Elimination of DG set usage by provision of 24×7 electricity to urban areas.**
- Primary mitigation: eliminate the need for DG sets through uninterrupted power supply.
- DG sets contribute 7–18% of ambient pollution in NACs; eliminating use is the most effective mitigation.

**Action 6: Evolve standards and norms for in-use DG sets below 800 KW category.**
- Currently, 91% of DG sets below 800 kW have no in-use emission regulations.
- CPCB to formulate notification on control of pollution from diesel generators in-use.

**Action 7: For DG sets already operational, mandate one of two compliance options:**
- **Option A:** Use retrofitted emission control equipment with a **minimum PM capturing efficiency of at least 70%**, type approved by one of 5 CPCB-recognized certification laboratories.
- **Option B:** Shift to gas-based generators — either new gas-based DG sets or retrofitting existing DG sets for partial gas usage (mixture of diesel and gas).
- Estimated cost of retrofit: less than 10% of the generator set cost.

**CPCB-recognized certification labs for DG set retrofit devices:**
1. Automotive Research Association of India (ARAI), Pune, Maharashtra
2. International Centre for Automotive Technology (ICAT), Manesar, Haryana
3. Indian Oil Corporation R&D Centre, Faridabad, Haryana
4. Indian Institute of Petroleum, Dehradun, Uttarakhand
5. Vehicle Research Development Establishment (VRDE), Ahmednagar, Maharashtra

### 3.4 Power Sector Controls

**Action 8: Stringent compliance by all Thermal Power Plants (TPPs) with emission norms.**
- TPP standards for PM, SOₓ, NOₓ, mercury, and water consumption notified under Environment (Protection) Amendment Rules, 2015 (07.12.2015).
- New stations: must meet norms from 01.01.2017.
- Existing stations: compliance plan extended to December 2022 for FGD installation and other pollution control equipment.
- All existing TPPs must comply per action plan issued December 2017 under EPA 1986.

**Action 9: Optimize use of natural gas / clean fuel-based thermal power plants; phase out older coal-based plants.**
- Prioritize capacity utilization of natural gas / clean fuel TPPs.
- Phase out older coal-based units; convert specific coal plants to natural gas where feasible.

**Action 10: Expand renewable power initiatives in non-attainment cities.**
- India target: 175 GW from renewables by 2022 (100 GW solar).
- National Solar Mission, National Mission for Enhanced Energy Efficiency to be leveraged for NACs.

**Action 11: Explore flyash utilization in 102 non-attainment cities.**
- Flyash from coal TPPs must be managed and utilized (in construction, cement, road base) to prevent fugitive dust.

### 3.5 Third-Party Audit (Gujarat Model)

**Action 12: Adopt third-party audit model for polluting industries.**
- Gujarat randomized evaluation: random assignment of third-party auditors, payment from common pool, monitoring auditors for accuracy, financial incentives for accurate reports.
- Outcomes: **80% less likelihood of false pollution readings**; industrial plants reduced air and water polluting emissions by **28%**.
- Recommend this model for all states for enhanced compliance.
