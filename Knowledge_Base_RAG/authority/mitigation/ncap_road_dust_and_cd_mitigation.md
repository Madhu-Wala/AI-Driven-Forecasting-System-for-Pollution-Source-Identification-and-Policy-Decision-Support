# NCAP Mitigation: Road Dust and Construction & Demolition (C&D) Dust

**Source:** National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) Report, Section 7.5.1, Pages 31–33  
**Authority:** Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEF&CC), CPCB  
**Category:** Authority — Road Dust and Construction Mitigation  

---

## 1. Source Description and Pollution Drivers

Road dust and dust from construction and demolition (C&D) activities are the **major contributors to particulate pollution in Indian cities**.

### Quantified Contribution (Delhi reference data — NCAP):

| Study | Dust Source | Contribution to PM₁₀ |
|-------|------------|----------------------|
| IIT Kanpur (2015) — Winter | Soil and road dust | 14.4% |
| IIT Kanpur (2015) — Summer | Soil and road dust | 26.5% |
| IIT Kanpur (2015) — Winter | Construction material | 3.1% |
| IIT Kanpur (2015) — Summer | Construction material | 4.1% |
| IIT Kanpur (Delhi, summer) | Total dust (soil, road, construction) | Up to **50% of PM₁₀** |

**Mumbai-specific (MPCB/CPCB urban source apportionment study — 6 cities 2007–2010):**
- Dust from road dust re-suspension, construction activities, and soil: **6%–58% of PM₁₀** across six major cities including Mumbai.

### Key Pollution Mechanisms:
- **Manual sweeping** with brooms blows more dust into the air than it removes — mechanical sweeping is essential.
- Collected dust dumped at landfills **returns to the city in wind** unless dampened or covered with green material — no standard SOP exists for dust disposal.
- Road dust re-suspension peaks in summer due to dry conditions and higher wind velocities.
- Construction activities generate fine PM₂.₅ from excavation, demolition, material transport, and exposed stockpiles.

## 2. Regulatory Framework

| Regulation | Scope | Requirement |
|------------|-------|-------------|
| C&D Waste Management Rules, 2016 | All construction/demolition sites | Recover, recycle, reuse C&D waste; segregate and deposit to collection centres for processing |
| C&D Waste Management Rules, 2016 | Local bodies | Utilize 10%–20% material from C&D waste in municipal and government contracts |
| Dust Mitigation Notification, January 2018 (EPA, 1986) | Infrastructural projects and demolition activities nationwide | Mandatory dust mitigation measures; MoEF&CC empowered to issue notices against local authorities and state agencies for non-implementation |
| Dust Mitigation Notification, 2018 | All applicable sites | 11-point dust mitigation measures inserted into the existing framework |

**Legal consequence of non-compliance:** MoEF&CC can issue notices against local authorities and state agencies directly. MPCB/SPCBs may close non-compliant construction sites.

## 3. Mitigation Measures and Action Points

### 3.1 Road Dust Control

**Action 1: Introduce mechanical sweepers on the basis of feasibility study in cities.**
- Mechanical sweepers must replace manual broom sweeping on high-traffic roads.
- Mechanical sweeping is more effective — brooms re-suspend fine particles; mechanical sweepers capture them.

**Action 2: Evolve SOP for disposal of collected dust from mechanical sweeping.**
- Current practice of dumping collected dust at landfills is ineffective — wind returns particles to the city.
- SOP must address: dampening dust before disposal, placement of green cover over disposal areas, prevention of re-suspension at disposal sites.

**Action 3: Wall-to-wall paving of roads to be mandated.**
- Unpaved road shoulders and edges are major re-suspension sources.
- Mandatory paving eliminates bare soil exposed to vehicle turbulence.

**Action 4: Sewage treatment plant (STP)-treated water sprinkling system along roads and at intersecting road junctions.**
- Spraying of water twice per day before peak traffic hours is very effective in reducing airborne dust load.
- STP-treated water should be used for road sprinkling (not potable water).
- Priority areas: arterial roads, junctions, high-traffic stretches, unpaved service roads.

**Action 5: Maintenance and repair of roads on priority.**
- Deteriorated road surfaces increase re-suspension.
- Priority maintenance of high-traffic arterials reduces fugitive dust generation.

**Action 6: Greening and landscaping of all major arterial roads and national highways.**
- Vegetation filter strips (100 m long, 5–12 m wide, three-storied community with 8–12 m high canopy) effectively trap dust and assimilate pollutants.
- Grassing of open spaces with native grasses prevents dust pollution.
- Identification of major polluting stretches required before implementation.

### 3.2 Construction and C&D Dust Control

**Action 7: Stringent implementation of C&D Waste Management Rules, 2016, and Dust Mitigation Notification, 2018.**
- Enforce: segregation of C&D waste, deposition to collection centres, recycling and reuse.
- Local bodies must utilize 10%–20% recycled C&D material in municipal/government projects.

**Action 8: Stringent control of dust from construction activities using enclosures, fogging machines, and barriers.**
- Construction site perimeter hoarding (solid barriers) must be installed.
- Fogging machines and misting systems to be deployed at active excavation and demolition areas.
- Material stockpiles to be covered with tarpaulin at all times.
- Wheel-wash facilities mandatory at construction site vehicle exits.
- Anti-smog guns mandatory at large sites (> 20,000 sq.m.).

## 4. Technical Specifications and Benchmarks

| Measure | Specification | Basis |
|---------|--------------|-------|
| Vegetation filter strips | 100 m long, 5–12 m wide, three-storied canopy at 8–12 m height | Effective for dust trapping and PAH assimilation |
| Road water sprinkling frequency | **Twice per day**, before peak traffic hours | NCAP recommendation; very effective in reducing airborne dust |
| Mechanical sweeper introduction | Based on city-specific feasibility study | To replace manual broom sweeping on major roads |
| C&D material recycling by local bodies | **10%–20%** of C&D waste in government contracts | C&D Rules, 2016 |
| Anti-smog gun deployment | Mandatory for sites **> 20,000 sq.m.** | Dust Mitigation Notification, 2018 |
