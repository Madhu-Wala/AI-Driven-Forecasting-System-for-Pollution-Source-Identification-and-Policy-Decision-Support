# NCAP Mitigation: Construction and Demolition (C&D) Dust

**Source:** National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) Report, Section 7.5.1, Pages 31–33  
**Authority:** MoEF&CC, CPCB, Municipal Corporations, SPCBs  
**Category:** Authority — Construction Dust Mitigation  

---

## 1. Source Description and Pollution Drivers

Construction and demolition activities are among the **top contributors to PM₁₀ and PM₂.₅** in Indian non-attainment cities, particularly in summer and wherever large infrastructure projects are active.

### Quantified Contribution:

**CPCB Study (2010) — 10 monitoring locations in Delhi:**
| Source | PM₁₀ Contribution |
|--------|-------------------|
| Construction | **22%–23.1%** |

**Mumbai-specific (CPCB/MoEF&CC 2007–2010 study across 6 cities including Mumbai):**
- Dust from road dust re-suspension, **construction activities**, and soil: **6%–58% of PM₁₀** in the 6 cities studied.
- Mumbai MPCB data: Construction activities contribute approximately **9%** of particulate matter directly.

**Seasonal dynamics:**
- Construction dust contribution is significant in both summer (dry conditions amplify re-suspension) and winter (ongoing construction with reduced natural dilution).
- Rise in high-rise construction in Mumbai is also **blocking sea breeze penetration**, reducing natural pollutant dispersal from coastal winds.

### Key Pollution Mechanisms:
- **Excavation and demolition** release coarse PM₁₀ and fine PM₂.₅ directly into the air.
- **Open stockpiles** of sand, aggregate, cement, and demolition debris are re-suspended by vehicle turbulence and wind.
- **Vehicle wheels tracking dust** onto paved roads from construction sites creates secondary road dust re-suspension.
- **Demolition debris transport** without covering releases fugitive PM on roads.
- **Uncovered scaffolding and building faces** during high-rise construction release fine cement and concrete dust.

## 2. Regulatory Framework

| Regulation | Key Requirements |
|------------|-----------------|
| C&D Waste Management Rules, 2016 (MoEF&CC) | Recover, recycle, reuse C&D waste; segregate and deposit to collection centres; local bodies must use 10%–20% recycled C&D material in government contracts |
| Dust Mitigation Notification, January 2018 (MoEF&CC, under EPA 1986) | Mandatory dust mitigation measures at all infrastructural projects and demolition activities nationwide; 11-point measures inserted; MoEF&CC may issue notices to local authorities and state agencies for non-implementation |
| CPCB Direction | Anti-smog guns mandatory at construction sites with area > 20,000 sq.m. |
| MPCB / SPCB authority | Construction sites found violating dust control norms may receive immediate stop-work notice and site closure |

## 3. Mitigation Measures — Complete Action List

### Site-Level Dust Control Measures

**Action 1: Stringent implementation of C&D Waste Management Rules, 2016, and Dust Mitigation Notification, 2018.**
- Full enforcement of both regulations at all construction and demolition sites.
- Waste generators (builders, contractors) responsible for segregating C&D waste and depositing to CPCB/municipal collection centres for processing.
- Local bodies to enforce 10%–20% recycled C&D material usage in municipal and government construction projects.

**Action 2: Stringent control of dust from construction activities using enclosures, fogging machines, and barriers.**
- **Perimeter hoarding (solid barriers):** All construction sites must have solid boundary enclosures to prevent dust escape at site perimeter.
- **Fogging machines and misting systems:** Deploy at active excavation zones, demolition areas, and material crushing operations.
- **Net covering on scaffolding:** All scaffolding on high-rise buildings must be covered with dust-retaining green nets.
- **Site boundary barriers:** Physical barriers at all vehicle entry/exit points.

**Action 3: Cover all material stockpiles.**
- Sand, aggregate, gravel, demolition debris, and construction material stockpiles must be covered with tarpaulin at all times when not actively in use.
- Uncovered stockpiles are a direct PM re-suspension source.

**Action 4: Install and operate wheel-washing facilities at all vehicle exit points.**
- All construction vehicles (trucks, JCBs, dumpers) must pass through a wheel-wash before exiting the site.
- Prevents construction dust from being tracked onto public roads, where it becomes road dust.

**Action 5: Deploy anti-smog guns for sites > 20,000 sq.m.**
- Anti-smog gun trucks must be stationed at large construction sites.
- Mandatory per CPCB direction; BMC enforcement applies in Mumbai.

**Action 6: Water sprinkling on construction site access roads and surrounding unpaved areas.**
- Water sprinkling twice daily before peak traffic hours on roads adjacent to active construction sites.
- Special attention to unpaved stretches used by construction vehicles.

### Demolition-Specific Measures

**Action 7: Pre-demolition planning for dust suppression.**
- Wet demolition (water spraying before and during demolition) required.
- Staged demolition preferred over simultaneous bulk demolition.
- Demolition debris to be covered and transported in sealed trucks.

**Action 8: No transport of C&D waste in uncovered vehicles.**
- All trucks transporting construction or demolition material on public roads must have covered loads.
- Enforcement: Traffic Police and RTO may intercept and penalize.

### Road Infrastructure Measures

**Action 9: Wall-to-wall paving of roads in areas adjacent to active construction zones.**
- Unpaved road shoulders near construction sites are major secondary dust sources.
- Mandatory paving of road surfaces including unpaved verges.

**Action 10: Maintenance and repair of roads around construction sites on priority.**
- Deteriorated road surfaces in construction zones increase re-suspension.
- Priority repair of access roads and arterials adjacent to major construction projects.

## 4. Compliance Enforcement Protocol (Mumbai Context)

**Inspection Process:**
1. BMC ward engineers and MPCB sub-regional officers conduct **joint inspection rounds** of all active construction sites.
2. Each site assessed against dust control compliance checklist.
3. Non-compliant sites receive **immediate stop-work notice.**

**Compliance checklist (per Dust Mitigation Notification 2018):**
- [ ] Water sprinkling/misting systems operational
- [ ] Stockpiles covered with tarpaulin
- [ ] Wheel-washing facility at vehicle exit
- [ ] Perimeter hoarding (dust barriers) intact
- [ ] Anti-smog gun deployed (if site > 20,000 sq.m.)
- [ ] Scaffolding net covering installed
- [ ] Workers wearing PPE (dust masks)
- [ ] C&D waste segregated and deposited to collection centre

**Mumbai GRAP enforcement data:** In November 2025, 53 out of 70 construction sites inspected (76%) were found violating dust-control norms — highlighting the scale of persistent non-compliance.

| Recycled C&D material use in government contracts | Municipal Corporation / PWD | State government departments |
| Dust Mitigation Notification enforcement | MoEF&CC (can issue notices to local authorities) | MPCB; Municipal Corporation |

## 6. Key Thresholds and Standards

| Parameter | Threshold / Standard |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Anti-smog gun requirement | Sites > **20,000 sq.m.** |
| Water sprinkling frequency | **Twice daily** before peak traffic hours |
| C&D material recycling by local bodies | **10%–20%** of C&D waste in government contracts |
| Construction PM₁₀ contribution (Delhi CPCB study) | **22%–23.1%** |
| Mumbai construction PM contribution (MPCB) | ~**9%** of particulate matter |
| Mumbai GRAP compliance rate (Nov 2025 inspections) | Only **24% compliant** (53/70 sites violated) |
