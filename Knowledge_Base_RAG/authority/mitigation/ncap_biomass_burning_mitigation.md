# NCAP Mitigation: Biomass Burning and Agricultural Emissions

**Source:** National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) Report, Sections 7.5.5 and 7.5.6, Pages 40–44  
**Authority:** Ministry of Agriculture (MoA), MoEF&CC, CPCB, ISRO, State Agriculture Departments  
**Category:** Authority — Biomass Burning Mitigation  

---

## 1. Source Description and Pollution Drivers

Biomass burning encompasses: agricultural crop residue burning (stubble burning), open burning of municipal solid waste, burning of leaves and organic material, and landfill fires. These are major episodic and chronic contributors to PM₂.₅, CO, and toxic pollutants.

### Quantified Emission Data:

**Paddy straw burning — emission factors per tonne burned:**
| Pollutant | Quantity Released per Tonne of Paddy Straw Burned |
|-----------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Particulate Matter (PM) | **3 kg** |
| Carbon Monoxide (CO) | **60 kg** |
| Carbon Dioxide (CO₂) | **1,460 kg** |
| Ash | **199 kg** |
| Sulphur Dioxide (SO₂) | **2 kg** |

**Stubble burning atmospheric impact:**
- CO₂ levels increase by **70%**
- Carbon monoxide concentration rises by **7%**
- Nitrogen dioxide concentration rises by **2.1%**

**Paddy straw nutrient value lost per tonne burned:**
| Nutrient | Loss per tonne burned |
|----------|----------------------|
| Nitrogen (N) | 5.5 kg |
| Phosphorus (P₂O₅) | 2.3 kg |
| Potassium (K₂O) | 25 kg |
| Sulphur (S) | 1.2 kg |
| Carbon | 400 kg |

**National scale burning:**
- Estimated **23 million tonnes** of paddy straw burned annually in Punjab, Haryana, and Uttar Pradesh.
- Burning window: September 20 – November 15 (harvest to rabi crop sowing; only 2–3 weeks available).

**Urban waste burning (Delhi/cities):**
- Biomass and open waste burning contribute **20–30% of total air pollution** in cities like Delhi.
- Open trash burning produces PM₂.₅ equivalent to **29% of officially reported global human-related PM₂.₅ emissions** (global study).
- Open waste fires also release: **10% of mercury** and **64% of PAHs (polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons)** of reported global emissions.

### IIT Kanpur Study (Delhi 2015) — Biomass Contribution:
| Pollutant | Season | Biomass Burning Contribution |
|-----------|--------|------------------------------|
| PM₁₀ | Winter | 16.7% |
| PM₁₀ | Summer | 6.8% |
| PM₂.₅ | Winter | **25.8%** |
| PM₂.₅ | Summer | 12.2% |

## 2. Regulatory Framework

| Regulation | Requirement |
|------------|-------------|
| MoEF&CC ban (existing) | Burning of leaves, biomass, and MSW is banned |
| National Policy for Management of Crop Residues (NPMCR), 2014 | Technical measures and central financial assistance for crop residue management |
| Central Sector Scheme 2018–2020 (100% Central Share) | Promotion of Agricultural Mechanization for In-Situ Management of Crop Residue in Punjab, Haryana, UP, Delhi NCT |
| National Biofuel Policy (NPB), 2018 | 20% ethanol blending in petrol; 5% biodiesel in diesel by 2030; crop residues included as feedstock |
| SWM Rules 2016 (CPCB directions) | Strict implementation against open MSW burning; bio-mining and bio-remediation of legacy waste |
| CPCB directions under Section 5 E(P) Act | Implementation of SWM Rules 2016 regarding fire incidents at MSW dumpsites |

**Central Sector Scheme Funding:**
- Total outlay: **₹1,151.80 crore** (FY 2018–19 to 2019–20)
- Released in FY 2018–19: **₹575.18 crore**
- Components: (i) Farm Machinery Banks for custom hiring; (ii) financial assistance to farmers for machinery procurement; (iii) information, education, and communication (IEC) for awareness.
- Nodal departments: State Departments of Agriculture of Punjab, Haryana, UP, Delhi NCT.

## 3. Agricultural Residue Burning — Mitigation Measures and Action Points

### In-Situ Management (Most Feasible — NCAP Recommendation)

**Action 1: In-situ use of paddy straw as mulch for wheat sowing and soil incorporation for potato/vegetable planting.**
- NCAP identifies this as the **most feasible and economical** option to begin with among all alternatives.
- Reduces burning need; maintains soil organic matter and nutrient content.

**Action 2: Evaluate status of implementation of the Central Sector Scheme and its impact on reducing crop residue burning.**
- Monitor scheme outcomes in Punjab, Haryana, UP, and Delhi NCT.
- Assess machine bank utilization and actual reduction in burning incidents.

### Ex-Situ Management Options (for scale-up)

**Action 3: Evaluate socio-economic feasibility of ex-situ crop residue conversion options:**
- Prali-Char (biochar from paddy straw)
- Biochar
- **Pellets and briquettes** — alternate fuel for brick kilns, industrial boilers, paper and packaging, coal-fired TPPs, transport sector
- BioCNG
- Bioethanol
- Assessment to be done in context of National Biofuel Policy (NPB) 2018 which includes crop residues as feedstock.

**Action 4: Extend crop residue burning mitigation initiatives:**
- Expand from NCR to all parts of India.
- Expand from paddy straw to sugarcane residue and other crop residues.

### Monitoring and Enforcement

**Action 5: Coordinate with ISRO for regular availability of Remote Sensing Monitoring data on crop burning.**
- Satellite-based Active Fire Event (AFE) monitoring to be made available regularly.
- Data to be shared with CPCB and State Agriculture Departments for enforcement coordination.

### Agricultural Ammonia Emissions

**Action 6: Evolve and implement plan for management of agricultural emissions from fertilizers and livestock waste.**
- Agricultural ammonia from fertilized fields and livestock waste combines with combustion pollutants (NOₓ, sulfates from vehicles, power plants, industries) to create secondary PM₂.₅ aerosols.
- Nitrous oxide from fertilizers is a major GHG and PM precursor.
- Strong R&D base required; R&D funding to be supported.

**Action 7: Strengthen capacity-building initiatives for Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVKs).**
- KVKs are frontline agricultural extension centres at district level.
- Strengthen KVK capacity to disseminate crop residue management best practices to farmers.

## 4. Municipal Solid Waste and Open Burning — Mitigation Measures and Action Points

The following apply specifically to waste-sector biomass burning:

**Action 8: Transform centralized waste disposal to decentralized sustainable system in 102 non-attainment cities.**
- Current landfill-based centralized system generates methane (50% of landfill gas), causes landfill fires, and necessitates open burning of overflow.
- Decentralized systems: local composting, biomethanation, C&D waste plants, wet/dry segregation at source.

**Action 9: Source segregation into dry and wet waste to be made mandatory.**
- Involvement of municipalities and Resident Welfare Associations (RWAs).
- Mandatory training and capacity building of municipalities and RWAs.

**Action 10: Transition to zero-waste pathway through integrated solid waste management.**
- Targeting: waste prevention → recycling → composting → energy recovery → treatment → disposal.
- Implement 'polluters pay' principle, recycling projects, composting, biomethanation, RDF (Refuse Derived Fuel) plants, and co-processing.

**Action 11: Construct decentralized composting plants, biomethanation plants, and C&D waste plants.**

**Action 12: Deploy fixed compactors; eliminate dhalaos (open waste collection points).**
- Dhalaos are open-air waste collection points where waste is frequently burned; replacing with sealed compactors eliminates burning.

**Action 13: Stringent implementation of six waste management rules:**
- Solid Waste Management Rules 2016
- Hazardous Waste Management Rules
- Electronic Waste (e-Waste) Management Rules
- Bio-medical Waste Management Rules
- Plastic Waste Management Rules
- C&D Waste Management Rules 2016

**Action 14: Promote technologies converting waste/plastic/MSW to energy (inline with National Biofuel Policy).**
- Waste-to-energy must use efficient, clean processes — not open burning or inefficient incinerators.
- Dioxins and furans (highly carcinogenic) are produced by inefficient incineration; must be avoided.
- Landfill gas (50% methane + 50% CO₂) can be captured for clean energy.
