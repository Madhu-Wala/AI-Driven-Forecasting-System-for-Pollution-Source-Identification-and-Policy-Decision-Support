## 1. Mumbai-Specific GRAP Context

Mumbai's GRAP is administered by the **Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC)** as the primary implementing municipal authority, in coordination with **MPCB Regional Office (Mumbai)**, **Mumbai Traffic Police**, and **Mumbai Metro Rail Corporation (MMRCL / MMRDA)**.

Mumbai's coastal geography historically provided natural pollutant dispersion through sea breezes. However, **high-rise construction has progressively blocked coastal wind penetration into inland suburbs**, reducing this natural buffer. As a result, suburbs such as Malad, Borivali East, Andheri East, Mulund West, Deonar, Mazgaon, Powai, and Navy Nagar now exhibit pollution patterns comparable to landlocked cities during winter stagnation episodes.

**Key Mumbai-specific pollution drivers that GRAP enforcement must target:**
- Construction dust (high-rise residential, Metro rail, coastal road, infrastructure projects)
- Vehicular congestion on narrow suburban roads and arterial corridors
- Open waste burning and landfill fires (Deonar landfill is a chronic PM source)
- Marine/port activities (unique to Mumbai; contributes ~18% of PM₁₀ and ~26% of PM₂.₅)
- Industrial units in suburban industrial zones (Vikhroli, Andheri MIDC, Thane border zones)
- DG set use in commercial buildings during power outages

## 2. GRAP Activation: Mumbai Decision Procedure

### Step 1: Continuous AQI Monitoring
- MPCB CAAQMS stations across Mumbai provide real-time 24-hour rolling PM₂.₅ and PM₁₀ data
- BMC Environmental Cell and MPCB Regional Office monitor SAFAR app forecasts daily during October–March (high-pollution season)
- Station clusters are monitored by ward/zone — GRAP may be activated for specific wards rather than the entire city if localized pollution is detected

### Step 2: Threshold Breach Identification
- When PM₂.₅ or PM₁₀ at one or more stations approaches or breaches a GRAP category threshold, the MPCB Regional Officer notifies the BMC Commissioner and Municipal Corporation environmental department
- SAFAR 24–48 hour forecast is checked to determine whether the breach is a momentary spike or a sustained pollution episode

### Step 3: Task Force Consultation
- City-Level Implementation Committee (Task Force) is notified
- Task Force reviews: current PM data, forecast data, number of stations in breach, meteorological conditions (wind speed, humidity, temperature inversion)
- If two or more monitoring stations within a zone breach a threshold simultaneously, zone-level GRAP activation is considered

### Step 4: GRAP Stage Invocation
- Task Force issues formal activation order specifying:
  - GRAP category activated
  - Geographic scope (city-wide or specific wards/zones)
  - Effective date and time
  - Responsible authorities notified
- Activation order is communicated to BMC ward offices, Traffic Police control room, MPCB enforcement teams, RTO, and Metro operations

### Step 5: Public Communication
- BMC and MPCB issue public advisory via official channels simultaneously with GRAP activation
- Media release to newspapers, TV, radio
- Social media posts on BMC and MPCB official accounts

## 3. Enforcement Operations: Mumbai GRAP Category I

### Construction Site Enforcement
**Operational protocol:**
1. BMC ward engineers and MPCB sub-regional officers conduct **joint inspection rounds** of all active construction sites in the affected zone
2. Each site is checked against a dust control compliance checklist:
   - Water sprinkling/misting systems operational
   - Stockpiles covered with tarpaulin
   - Wheel-washing facility at vehicle exit
   - Perimeter dust barriers (hoarding) intact
   - Anti-smog gun deployed (mandatory for sites > 20,000 sq.m.)
   - Site boundary net covering installed on scaffolding
3. Non-compliant sites receive **immediate stop-work notice**
4. Compliance record: During November 2025 GRAP enforcement in Mumbai, **53 out of 70 sites inspected (76%) were found violating dust-control norms** — highlighting the scale of enforcement required

**Responsibility split:**
- MPCB: Issues environmental non-compliance notices; authority to close sites under EP Act
- BMC: Issues building plan-related stop-work orders; controls permission reactivation

### Garbage Burning Enforcement
**Operational protocol:**
1. BMC solid waste management teams conduct ward-level patrols
2. Waste burning incidents reported via complaint apps (Aaple Sarkar, BMC 1916 helpline) are prioritized for same-day response
3. Heavy fines imposed on individuals caught burning waste
4. Landfill fire incidents (especially Deonar) require MPCB involvement for large-scale fire suppression and MPCB penalty proceedings against responsible contractor

### Road Sweeping and Dust Suppression
**Operational protocol:**
1. BMC mechanized sweeping vehicles operate on programmed route schedules
2. Under GRAP Category I: scheduling is intensified on identified high-dust roads
3. Water tankers perform sprinkling on arterial roads; special attention to unpaved stretches (service roads near construction sites)
4. Anti-smog gun trucks deployed at major construction corridors and industrial zones

### PUC Enforcement
**Operational protocol:**
1. Traffic Police and RTO conduct **joint PUC checking drives** at key intersections and near vehicle-dense commercial areas
2. Vehicles without valid PUC certificates are challaned
3. Commercial vehicles (autos, taxis, buses) are given priority for checking as they operate high daily kilometres
4. Polluting commercial vehicles identified for fitness retesting at RTO

## 4. Enforcement Operations: Mumbai GRAP Category II

### DG Set Enforcement
**Operational protocol:**
1. MPCB Sub-Regional Officer (Mumbai) issues public circular notifying DG set prohibition
2. MPCB inspection teams visit commercial complexes, malls, hospitals (for non-emergency DG), office buildings, and industrial estates
3. DG sets found running without emergency justification are sealed
4. Hospitals, emergency services, and data centres seeking exemption must obtain written Task Force approval

### Parking Fee Enhancement
**Operational protocol:**
1. BMC Deputy Commissioner (Traffic/Parking) issues order to all municipal parking contractors
2. Fee boards at all BMC parking lots updated
3. Private parking operators are notified; voluntary compliance encouraged; enforcement follows for non-compliance
4. Enhanced fee applies to all zones including CBD (Nariman Point, BKC, Andheri East commercial zone), residential zones, and suburban commercial areas

### Public Transport Augmentation
**Operational protocol — Buses:**
1. BEST (Brihanmumbai Electric Supply and Transport) operations department deploys additional buses on high-demand routes
2. Contract buses procured from private operators under pre-agreed emergency contracts
3. Real-time bus tracking data used to identify highest-demand routes for priority augmentation
4. AC bus services on key inter-suburb routes may be temporarily supplemented with additional regular buses

**Operational protocol — Metro:**
1. MMRCL / MMRDA operations department increases train frequency on operational lines (Metro Line 1: Versova–Andheri–Ghatkopar; Metro Line 2A, 7, and other operational lines)
2. Off-peak frequency gap reduced
3. Platform screen door operations and crowd management deployed for increased passenger volumes

### Health Advisory Dissemination
**Operational protocol:**
1. MPCB Regional Officer (Mumbai) drafts health advisory in coordination with BMC Health Department
2. Advisory distributed to:
   - Major city newspapers (English + Marathi)
   - Mumbai Doordarshan and private news channels
   - All-India Radio (AIR) Mumbai
   - BMC and MPCB social media handles
   - WhatsApp alerts to registered users of BMC/MPCB alert systems
3. Advisory content includes: current AQI, health risk level, specific advice for respiratory/cardiac patients, recommended actions, helpline numbers

## 5. Enforcement Operations: Mumbai GRAP Category III

### Industrial Unit Closure — Mumbai Context
**Priority closure list for Mumbai under Category III:**
- **Brick kilns**: Primarily located in peripheral Mumbai Metropolitan Region (MMR) areas
- **Hot mix plants**: Active near road construction projects; must be shut immediately
- **Stone crushers**: Located in Thane–Mumbai border zones; MPCB to coordinate with Thane Regional Office
- **Thermal power plants**: Trombay Thermal Power Station (TATA Power) — emissions to be reduced/curtailed; MPCB to issue emergency directions
- Other high-emission units identified in MPCB's source emission inventory for Mumbai

**Operational protocol:**
1. MPCB Regional Officer (Mumbai) issues closure orders to identified units within 4 hours of Category III activation
2. Units that fail to comply face immediate plant shutdown by MPCB enforcement team
3. Closure is maintained until GRAP Category III is formally de-escalated

### Intensified Road Cleaning
**Operational protocol:**
1. BMC shifts mechanized sweeping from scheduled to demand-based, with minimum daily sweeping mandated on all major roads
2. Water sprinkling frequency increased to 3–4 times per day on arterial roads
3. Additional water tankers deployed from BMC water supply department to support road sprinkling operation

## 6. Enforcement Operations: Mumbai GRAP Category IV (Emergency)

### Flying Squad Deployment (Mumbai Model)
BMC's Mumbai GRAP Category IV enforcement uses **flying squads** as the primary enforcement mechanism:
- Composition: BMC engineers + Traffic Police personnel + GPS-enabled patrol vehicles
- Mandate: Inspect every ward for compliance with all active GRAP restrictions
- Authority: Issue on-the-spot notices; escalate closure orders
- Reporting: Daily enforcement report compiled and submitted to BMC Commissioner and Task Force

### Truck and Heavy Vehicle Restriction
**Entry checkpoints for Mumbai:**
- Eastern Express Highway entry points (LBS Road junction, Thane check nakas)
- Western Express Highway entry points (Dahisar toll naka area)
- NH-48 / Eastern Freeway entry points
- Key cargo routes (Wadala, Govandi, Chembur industrial entry)
- Port Trust (Mumbai Port and Nhava Sheva) access roads — coordinate with Mumbai Port Authority

**Exempted categories (minimum exemptions):**
- Ambulances and fire brigade vehicles
- Vehicles carrying essential commodities (milk, vegetables, medicines, hospital supplies)
- Funeral vehicles
- Government emergency vehicles
- All other vehicles including private trucks, building material carriers, container trucks: PROHIBITED

### Construction Activity Halt
**Scope of construction halt in Mumbai:**
- All BMC-permitted building construction projects
- MMRDA and Metro rail construction sites (subject to Task Force exemption request for critical safety works)
- NHAI and PWD road projects within city limits
- Port-related construction activities
- Note: Underground utility repair (burst water main, sewage emergency) may be exempted at BMC Commissioner's discretion for public safety

### Odd-Even Vehicle Scheme
**Implementation framework for Mumbai:**
1. Traffic Police issue notification through official channels 12–24 hours before scheme takes effect (allowing commuters to plan)
2. Enforcement points established at entry/exit of major corridors (Worli, BKC, Andheri East, CBD)
3. Mobile enforcement teams deployed on arterial roads
4. Challaning authority: Traffic Police and Motor Vehicle Inspector (RTO) on deputation
5. Exemptions (Task Force to minimise): Emergency vehicles, public transport, vehicles with disability permits, vehicles carrying medical patients, two-wheelers if Task Force decides

## 7. Monitoring and AQI Tracking During Active GRAP

**Real-time monitoring during GRAP:**
- MPCB CAAQMS data checked every hour during active GRAP stages
- SAFAR app monitoring for 24–48 hour AQI forecast
- BMC Environmental Cell and MPCB Regional Office coordinate daily status calls
- Task Force receives daily AQI briefing including: current station readings, 24-hour average PM₂.₅/PM₁₀, forecast AQI trend, enforcement actions completed, violations detected and penalized

**De-escalation criteria:**
- PM₂.₅ and PM₁₀ must return below the lower threshold of the active category
- Must remain below threshold for at least 24 hours before de-escalation is considered
- 15-day minimum must have elapsed from date of invocation
- Task Force formally approves de-escalation; lower-stage actions remain active

Based on monitoring data and source attribution, the following Mumbai zones require priority GRAP enforcement:

| Zone / Area | Primary Pollution Sources | Priority GRAP Actions |

| Chakala Andheri East (K/E Ward) | Industrial, vehicular, construction | Industrial compliance; traffic management |
| Mazgaon (E Ward) | Port activities, industrial, vehicular | Port emission coordination; industrial enforcement |
| Mulund West (T Ward) | Vehicular (highway), construction | Traffic management; construction enforcement |
| Powai (L Ward) | Construction (SEEPZ area), vehicular | Construction site enforcement |
