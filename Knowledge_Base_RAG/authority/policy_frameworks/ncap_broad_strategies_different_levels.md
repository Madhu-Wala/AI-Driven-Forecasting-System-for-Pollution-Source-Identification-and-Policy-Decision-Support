# NCAP Broad Strategies at Different Levels

**Source:** National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) Report, Section 10 (Page 62) and Sections 7.4, 7.6, 7.7 (Pages 30–31, 45–47)  
**Authority:** MoEF&CC, CPCB, State Governments, Municipal Bodies  
**Category:** Authority — Policy Frameworks  

---

## 1. Strategic Framework Overview

The NCAP recognizes that **air quality management requires coordinated action at multiple geographic and institutional levels**. A single-level approach is insufficient because pollution sources, meteorological dynamics, and governance structures operate at different scales simultaneously.

**Five levels of air quality management identified:**
1. **Local** (source-level)
2. **City** (municipal/urban)
3. **State** (state government and SPCB)
4. **Regional** (inter-state, Indo-Gangetic Plain)
5. **Transboundary** (international/South Asia)

---

## 2. Local Level Strategies

Local-level strategies target **specific emission sources** and are implemented primarily by municipal corporations and enforcement agencies.

### Dust Management (Local)
- Mechanical sweeping on high-traffic roads.
- Water sprinkling twice daily before peak traffic hours on paved and unpaved roads.
- STP-treated water for sprinkling (conservation of potable water).
- Mandatory dust mitigation at all construction and demolition sites.
- C&D waste collection, segregation, and recycling.
- Wall-to-wall paving of roads to eliminate bare soil re-suspension.

### Waste Management (Local)
- Source segregation of dry and wet waste at household level.
- Decentralized composting, biomethanation, and C&D waste processing.
- Prohibition and enforcement of open burning of leaves, biomass, MSW.
- Deployment of sealed compactors; elimination of open dhalaos.
- Landfill fire prevention and management (bio-mining and bio-remediation of legacy waste).

### Traffic Management (Local)
- Traffic police deployment at high-congestion junctions.
- PUC enforcement drives — joint RTO + Traffic Police.
- Smooth traffic flow at busy intersections reduces idling-related emissions.

### Industry (Local)
- Enforcement of emission standards at individual industrial units.
- OCEMS data monitoring and compliance enforcement.
- Closure of non-compliant units.
- DG set emission control (retrofit or gas conversion).

### Plantation (Local)
- Plantation at pollution hotspots: traffic junctions, industrial zones, footpaths, dust-prone areas.
- Vegetation filter strips (100 m long, 5–12 m wide, three-storied canopy at 8–12 m) along major roads.
- Biodiversity parks as non-point source pollution filters.

## 3. City Level Strategies

City-level strategies require **integrated urban management** across municipal departments, transport authorities, and pollution control boards.

### City Action Plans (CAPs)
- **Mandatory for all 102 Non-Attainment Cities (NACs).**
- CAPs must be guided by science-based, source-apportionment-informed approach.
- Preliminary CAPs to be formulated for all 102 NACs.
- CAPs to be taken up for implementation by State Governments and city administrations.
- Priority: State capitals and cities with population > 1 million.
- Phased approach: first phase covers 10 cities with IITs, NEERI, TERI, ARAI support.

**CAP components:**
1. Strengthening of pollution monitoring mechanism
2. Source-wise cause analysis for air quality
3. Progress tracking on action plan and compliance with statutory guidelines/rules
4. Performance-based financial support from NCAP

**NCAP funding for CAPs:**
- Performance-based financial support linked to PM₁₀ reduction achievement.
- Funds sourced from: Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban), AMRUT, Smart City Mission, SATAT, FAME-II, Nagar Van Yojna, and State/UT Government resources.
- **Smart cities integration**: 43 of the 102 NACs are also Smart Cities — NCAP launched through Smart City framework in these cities.

### City Transport Strategy
- Review and extend Mass Rapid Transit (MRT) in each city.
- City-specific EV action plans; charging infrastructure in 102 cities.
- Congestion management — bypass routes, expressways, traffic signal optimization.

### City Monitoring Network
- Expand CAAQMS coverage in each city.
- Emergency Response System (GRAP) activated for AQI-based action triggers.
- Public Grievance Redressal Portal (PGRP) for citizen reporting of pollution violations.

## 4. State Level Strategies

### State Action Plans for Air Pollution (Section 7.7)

Each State Government is required to formulate and implement a **State Action Plan for Air Pollution** covering:
- Source apportionment analysis at state level
- State-level emission inventory
- Identification of major emission sources by sector
- Source-wise interventions and regulatory measures
- Timelines and agency assignments for each action

**Key state-level actions:**
1. Enforcement of emission norms through SPCBs for all industrial units.
2. Implementation of crop residue management schemes in agricultural states.
3. Coordinating city-level municipal action plans with state transport, industry, and agriculture departments.
4. SPCB enforcement of OCEMS at 17 categories of highly polluting industries.
5. Environment quality monitoring in all Critically Polluted Areas (CPAs) through third-party agencies.
6. Placing CEPI scores and environmental quality data in public domain.
7. State-level inter-ministerial coordination for multi-sectoral action.

### State Institutional Framework
- SPCBs/PCCs: primary state-level regulatory authority for industrial emissions, ambient monitoring, and enforcement.
- State Transport Departments: vehicle emission standards enforcement, MRT expansion, fleet modernization.
- State Agriculture Departments: crop residue management schemes.
- Urban Development Departments: city action plan coordination.

## 5. Regional Level Strategies (Inter-State Coordination)

Regional strategies apply particularly to the **Indo-Gangetic Plain (IGP)**, where pollutant transport across state boundaries is significant.

### Regional Action Points

**Transport (Regional):**
- Implement stringent auto-fuel policy and emission norms across all IGP states jointly.
- Road-to-rail and road-to-waterway modal shift for freight.
- Fleet modernization across state borders.
- Electric vehicle policies across the region.
- Clean fuels (CNG, gas, biofuels) — regional availability.
- Bypass roads and taxation policies to reduce urban through-traffic.

**Industry (Regional):**
- Stringent industrial emission standards applied uniformly across IGP states.
- Clean fuels mandate for industrial units (PNG, gas).
- Clean technology adoption incentives across the region.
- Continuous monitoring enforcement across state boundaries for industrial units with transboundary influence.

**Biomass (Regional):**
- Enhanced LPG penetration for cooking across rural IGP states.
- Agricultural residue burning control through coordinated state policies.
- Regional crop residue management program extending from paddy straw to all crops.

**Regional Plan Formulation:**
- Comprehensive Regional Plan to be formulated incorporating source apportionment study results.
- Regional source apportionment studies required to quantify inter-state transport contributions.

## 6. Transboundary Level Strategies

**Action 1: Link NDC forest and tree cover targets to NCAP.**
- NDC target: additional forest and tree cover of 2.5–3 billion tonnes of CO₂ equivalent by 2030.
- Focus on western regions (Rajasthan, Gujarat) for enhanced tree cover to reduce transboundary dust.
- Tree cover barriers reduce wind-blown dust entering from desert regions and neighboring countries.

**Action 2: Integrate UNCCD initiatives for transboundary dust.**
- United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD) initiatives to be integrated for addressing transboundary dust transport.

**Action 3: South-Asia regional air quality management.**
- Activate 'Male Declaration on Control and Prevention of Air Pollution and its Likely Transboundary Effects for South Asia.'
- Engage South Asia Co-operative Environment Programme (SACEP) for regional air quality coordination.

**Action 4: Formulate a comprehensive Transboundary Plan.**

## 8. Three-Tier Monitoring, Assessment, and Inspection System

To address the core problem of **lack of regular monitoring and inspection** (identified as the primary reason for non-compliance), NCAP mandates a three-tier system:

**Tier 1 — Real-Time Physical Data Collection:**
- Mobile app (smartphone-based) for field inspectors and third-party verifiers.
- Captures: GPS location, industry type, monitored parameters, photographic evidence.
- Operated by third-party verifiers in coordination with SPCBs.

**Tier 2 — Data Archiving and Analytics:**
- Central data archive.
- High-quality IT analytics companies corroborate data, verify authenticity against industry standards, historical data, and utility bills (electricity, water).

**Tier 3 — Action Trigger System:**
- Pre-defined actions triggered automatically based on verified data.
- Minimal human interface.
- Predefined action protocols for each data scenario.

**Action Points:**
1. Web-based three-tier system to be developed with NIC and relevant national and international agencies.
2. Adequate trained manpower for monitoring and inspection.
3. Intensive training of all stakeholders.
4. Mandatory use in all 102 non-attainment cities.

## 9. NCAP Targets and Performance Benchmarks

| Parameter | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| PM₂.₅ and PM₁₀ reduction target | **20%–30% reduction** from 2017 baseline by 2024 |
| Revised NCAP target (2022 update) | **40% reduction in PM₁₀** or reach 60 µg/m³ national standard by 2025–26 |
| Number of NACs covered | 102 (original) → 131 (revised) |
| States covered | 24 |
| Smart Cities in NAC list | 43 of the 102 NACs are Smart Cities |
| NCAP plan tenure | 5 years (2019–2024), extendable to 20–25 years |
| International benchmark (China 2013–17) | PM₂.₅ reduced by 33.3% in 5 years in 74 cities |
| International benchmark (Beijing/Seoul) | 35%–40% PM₂.₅ reduction in 5 years |
| Long-term benchmark (Santiago/Mexico City) | 61%–73% reduction over 22–25 years |
