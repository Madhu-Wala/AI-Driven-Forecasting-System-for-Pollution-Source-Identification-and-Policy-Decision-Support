## 1. Non-Attainment Cities (NAC) Framework

As per CPCB criteria, cities exceeding annual National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS) for any one notified parameter consecutively for five years (with minimum three monitoring stations) are classified as **Non-Attainment Cities (NACs)**.

CPCB has identified **123 Non-Attainment Cities** exceeding NAAQS, including coastal cities:
- Mumbai, Navi Mumbai, Vasai Virar, Thane (Maharashtra)
- Surat (Gujarat)
- Vishakhapatnam, Srikakulam, Ongole, Nellore (Andhra Pradesh)
- Thoothukudi (Tamil Nadu)
- Bhubaneswar, Balasore (Odisha)

**City Action Plans (CAPs)** have been prepared and rolled out for all non-attainment cities, with short, medium, and long-term actions targeting:
- Soil and Road Dust
- Vehicles
- Domestic Fuel
- MSW Burning
- Construction Material
- Industries

## 2. National Clean Air Programme (NCAP)

**Launched:** January 2019, Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC)

**Objectives:**
- Improve air quality in **131 cities** (non-attainment cities and Million Plus Cities) across 24 States
- Original target: 20–30% reduction in PM₁₀ concentration over 2017 baseline by 2024
- Revised target: Achieve up to **40% reduction in PM₁₀** or reach national standards (60 µg/m³) by 2025–26

**Implementation:**
- City Action Plans (CAPs) prepared by all 131 cities and implemented by Urban Local Bodies
- Performance-based financial support provided for implementation
- Funding sources integrated from: Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban), AMRUT, Smart City Mission, SATAT, FAME-II, Nagar Van Yojna, and State/UT government resources
- Public Grievance Redressal Portal (PGRP)/helpline developed by all 131 cities
- Emergency Response System (ERS/GRAP) developed by all 131 cities

**Outcomes:** 88 out of 131 cities showed improvement in PM₁₀ concentrations in FY 2022–23 vs. FY 2017–18 baseline.

## 3. Measures for Control of Vehicular Emissions

### Fuel Standards Upgrade
- **Leapfrogged from BS-IV to BS-VI** fuel standards:
  - Implemented in NCT of Delhi from April 1, 2018
  - Rolled out across rest of India from April 1, 2020
- Introduction of **BS-VI compliant vehicles** across the country since April 2020

### Electric Vehicle Promotion
- Department of Heavy Industry providing subsidy on e-vehicles under **FAME-II (Faster Adoption and Manufacture of Hybrid & Electric Vehicles)** scheme

### Alternative Fuels
- **SATAT (Sustainable Alternative Towards Affordable Transportation)**: initiative to set up Compressed Bio-Gas (CBG) production plants and make CBG available for automotive fuels

### Traffic Management
- **RFID (Radio-Frequency Identity) system** implemented for toll and Environment Compensation Charges on commercial vehicles entering Delhi
- Operationalization of Expressways and Highways to divert non-destined traffic

### Vehicular Refueling Emissions
- **Vapour Recovery System (VRS)** installation mandated in petrol pumps:
  - Selling gasoline > 100 kl/month in million plus cities
  - Selling > 300 kl/month in cities with population between 1 lakh to 1 million
- VRS directions issued to IOCL, BPCL, HPCL, RIL, Shell, and Nayara

## 4. Measures for Control of Industrial Emissions

### Emission Standards
- SO₂ and NOx emission standards notified for Thermal Power Plants
- Industry-specific environmental standards notified for **79 industrial sectors** (including emission standards for 56 sectors)

### Online Continuous Emission Monitoring System (OCEMS)
- CPCB directed all **17 categories of highly polluting industries** to install OCEMS
- 4,315 units under 17 categories; 3,734 units have installed OCEMS
- Closure directions still in force for 581 non-compliant units
- OCEMS installed in **red category air polluting industries** in Delhi-NCR

### Fuel Switching
- **Ban on use of pet coke and furnace oil** as fuel in NCR States since October 24, 2017
- Ban on use of imported pet coke in the country since July 26, 2018 (exceptions for permitted processes)
- Industrial units in Delhi shifted to PNG/cleaner fuels
- Operational units in NCR shifted to PNG/Biomass

### Brick Kilns
- Shifting of all operational **brick kilns to zig-zag technology** in Delhi and NCR

### DG Sets
- CPCB developed system for Emission Compliance Testing of Retro-fit Emission Control Devices (RECD) for Diesel Power Generating Sets up to 800 kW
- CPCB funding retrofitment/upgradation of DG sets in Government hospitals under EPC funds

## 5. Measures for Control of Stubble Burning

### Machinery Subsidy Scheme
- MoA&FW launched scheme in 2018 for subsidised crop residue management machinery and Custom Hiring Centres (CHCs) in Delhi, Punjab, Haryana, and Uttar Pradesh
- Total fund released 2018–2022: **₹2,440.07 crores**
- Over **2 lakh crop residue machineries** delivered to farmers and CHCs
- Over **39,000 CHCs** established

### Pelletization and Torrefaction Plants
- CPCB guidelines for one-time financial assistance for paddy straw-based pelletization plants:
  - Maximum ₹28 lakhs or 40% of capital cost for 1 TPH pelletization plant (max total ₹1.4 crore/proposal)
  - Maximum ₹56 lakhs or 40% of capital cost for 1 TPH torrefaction plant (max total ₹2.8 crore/proposal)
  - Corpus of ₹50 crores earmarked; expected yield: over 0.5 million metric tonnes of pellets annually
  - 9 plants sanctioned (7 in Punjab, 1 in Haryana, 1 in UP)
- CPCB addendum: one-time financial assistance to Municipal Corporations/Councils/Zilla Parishads in Punjab, Haryana, Delhi-NCR, UP, and Rajasthan for paddy straw briquetting plants

### Monitoring and Enforcement
- **Daily monitoring of Active Fire Events (AFEs)** during stubble burning period; reports shared with Commission on Air Quality Management (CAQM)
- 33 CPCB scientists deployed as flying squads for enforcement monitoring across 22 Punjab districts and 11 Haryana districts during stubble burning season (November 2023)
- CAQM directions: permitting PNG or biomass as industrial fuel in NCR; co-firing 5–10% biomass with coal in thermal power plants within 300 km of Delhi

## 6. Measures for MSW and Construction & Demolition Waste

### Guidelines Published by CPCB
- Environmental Management of Construction & Demolition (C&D) Wastes — March 2017
- Guidelines on Dust Mitigation Measures in Handling Construction Material and C&D Wastes — November 2017

### Enforcement Actions
- CPCB directed all SPCBs/PCCs to deploy **Anti-Smog Guns** and implement dust mitigation measures at construction projects/sites with area more than **20,000 sq. meters**
- CPCB directions under Section 5 of E(P) Act for implementation of SWM Rules 2016 with reference to fire incidents at MSW dumpsites
- Disposal of legacy waste by **bio-mining and bio-remediation** to address open burning and landfill fires
- CPCB funding NCR ULBs for road construction/repair and procurement of anti-smog guns and **mechanical road sweepers** under EPC funds

### Technical Research
- Dust suppressant research: approximately **30% reduction in dust concentration** observed up to 6 hours after application; advisory issued to State Boards for use of dust suppressant along with water on unpaved roads, heavy traffic roads, and construction sites

## 7. Air Quality Monitoring Network

### National AQI System
- **National Air Quality Index (AQI) launched in 2015**
- Information disseminated through daily AQI bulletins

### Monitoring Infrastructure
- National network: **1,447 ambient air quality monitoring stations** (516 continuous + 931 manual)
- Coverage: 516 cities across 28 states and 7 UTs

### Central Control Room (CPCB)
- Hour-to-hour tracking of PM concentrations, live AQI data, live monitoring station data
- **Air Quality Forecast available for Delhi-NCR**
- Daily AQI Bulletin published after analysis of multiple parameters

### CPCB Daily Reports Include:
- AQI of Delhi and NCR towns
- Comparative AQI status
- Year-wise trends of PM concentration
- Pollution hotspots for the day
- Active Fire Event (AFE) counts
- Contribution of stubble burning
- Meteorological forecast (inputs from IMD, SAFAR, IARI)

## 8. Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP)

- **GRAP prepared for implementation under different AQI categories** per Supreme Court Order dated December 2, 2016
- Revised GRAP published by CAQM on August 5, 2022; came into effect October 1, 2022
- GRAP revised again on October 6, 2023
- CPCB is a member of the sub-committee responsible for invoking GRAP provisions

## 9. Ground-Level Monitoring and Enforcement

- **CPCB deploying dedicated field teams during winter season from 2017 onwards** to check on-ground air pollution activities
- From December 3, 2021: **40 CPCB officers deployed as flying squads** for incognito inspection of industries, construction sites in Delhi-NCR
- Flying squad findings trigger CAQM actions including closure directions
- CPCB SAMEER app and social media (Twitter/Facebook) channels for public complaint redressal

## 10. Regulatory Actions and Directions

- CAQM directions covering:
  - Implementation of RECD system/dual fuel kits in DG sets
  - Use of cleaner fuels in industries
  - Shift to EV/CNG/BS-VI diesel in transport sector
  - Implementation of dust control measures at C&D sites
- Policy to curb air pollution in NCR formulated
